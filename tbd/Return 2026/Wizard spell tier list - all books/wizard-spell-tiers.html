How to read the tiers, and where the data comes from
SDefines the level. Take it whenever you can and memorize it most days.
AExcellent. Earns a slot on most adventuring days, or is a must-have in the book.
BGood, but situational, or outshone by a spell doing a similar job.
CNiche. Real uses, but you rarely memorize it.
DRarely worth a slot. Odd cases or flavor.
FA trap, or close to useless as written.
UUnranked: the wiki text is a stub with no mechanics.

CT is casting time, which in 2e adds to initiative: a hit before the spell goes off spoils it. Saves are set by the target, not the caster, so spells with no save or with a save penalty rank higher than they would in later editions.

Setting-bound and kit-restricted spells (Red Wizards, necromancers, the Seven Sisters, wu jen, Maztican hishna and pluma mages) are ranked as if the caster qualifies. The note names the restriction.

The list and texts come from the adnd2e fandom wiki's All Wizard Spells, a transcription of the books. Where it and a book disagree, the book wins. Not included: the Wizard's Spell Compendium's cantrips and psionic enchantments, which the wiki list leaves out, and Karsus's avatar, a 12th-level spell the wiki files at 1st. Every digit in a note appears in that spell's text.

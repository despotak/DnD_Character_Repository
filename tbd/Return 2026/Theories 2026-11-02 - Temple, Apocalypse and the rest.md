# Theories: the Temple, the Apocalypse and the rest (2026-11-02)

**Private, like the deep dive.** Each theory says whether it is **shareable** or **silent**, by the
deep dive's §6 rule: a deduction the characters can make from what they heard, plus lore they'd
know, is shareable; a reading of Panagiotis's real-world sources, or a guess at a twist we'd rather
he sprang on us, stays silent. The best shareable ones are in Reply 2 §3.

Tags as in the deep dive: **[T]** a text · **[S]** the table (NOTES) · **[C]** canon · **[I]** mine ·
**[P]** Panagiotis or Christos said it. Handout tags are the deep dive's (APO, CK, MAL, WAR, AUC,
SEL, WGV, THA) and the web's (OGH, ELF, BHA, LEG).

New canon read for this file: *Netheril: Empire of Magic* (2e), Jergal's entry; *Faiths & Avatars*
(2e) on Ilmater, Myrkul and Shar; FRCS 3e on Shar and the Yellow Rose.

## 1. Jergal is counting — *strong*

The best find, so it goes first.

[C] Jergal as Netheril knew him (*Netheril: Empire of Magic*):

- Titles: **Lord of the End of Everything**, Protector of the Names of the Dead, Scribe of the
  Doomed. His portfolio includes **dusk**.
- With his gaze he "could learn the sum total of a being's life, joys, fears, acts, and ultimate
  demise", and "simply by inscribing a mortal's name on his voluminous scroll, he could inflict a
  being's fated demise immediately". He speaks "with a disembodied, chilling voice".
- His church: "Life should be prolonged only when it served the greater cause of the death of the
  world."
- **The Night of Another Year.** On the last night of the year his priests read aloud "every name
  whose death they had recorded over the past year. When the last name was intoned", they cried
  *"One year ended; one year closer."*
- His realm is the Bone Castle, in the **Gray Waste**. Shar is his ally; he "formally courted her
  on several occasions".

Beside the campaign:

| The campaign | Jergal [C] |
|---|---|
| "When the last name is spoken, I shall take the breath. Not before." The third voice near Hedrack, 4th century (CK) | "When the last name was intoned…", "one year closer" |
| "He who ends all things awaits the last breath", carved in the abbey crypt [S Sat 6:53] | Lord of the End of Everything |
| ***THE DEAD HAVE BEEN READ***, behind the altar in Ancelm's dream (WGV) | the rite is reading the dead aloud |
| A **Grey** Pact between "a voice of endings" and "a knife of necessity" (CK) | a disembodied voice, and a realm in the Gray Waste; the knife is Bhaal, whom the letter calls the god of necessary murder |
| Irildë's **Heir of Dusk**, "Whose breath stills light, and whose eyes drink fate" (OGH) | dusk is in his portfolio, and his gaze reads a being's fate |
| No true name, no page in "the endless notebook" [S Sun 2:04] | he ends a life by writing its name |

[I] Savas guessed Jergal at the table [S Sat 6:54]. The canon turns the guess into a match: one
speaker behind both inscriptions. The Gray Waste reading of "Grey" is the weak row; the rest are
near-verbatim.

Three theories follow from it.

**1a. Hedrack keeps coming back because he can't be filed.** *Medium.* Jergal ends a life by
writing its name. Hedrack's name was "probably stripped from him" [S Sun 2:04], and he reappears
every time the Temple rises: *"It can't be chance… Something escapes us"* [S Sun 1:00]. If a death
is a name in a scroll, a man with no name has nowhere to be written, and his deaths don't hold.
That answers the DM's own question. It is Dahast's danger as much as his protection: the same
blank page. **Shareable**, as Dahast's fear. It is also the honest way to ask "protection or
danger?".

**1b. The abbey was holding a Night of Another Year.** *Medium.* "The dead have been read"; names
and words rising as smoke "over the altar"; *"They are feeding him"* (WGV). That is the rite, used to
feed something. If "him" is the one who ends all things, reading the dead brings the last name
nearer. If it is Nebiros, it's memory (deep dive §3.8). Either way, the Redactors' and the
Bhaalists' burning of names is not vandalism. It's arithmetic. **Shareable**, as Ancelm's reading:
an Oghmanite with his lore would know Jergal's old rite.

**1c. The Heir of Dusk is heir to a death god.** *Medium.* [C] Dusk was Jergal's, and in 2e it is
in Myrkul's portfolio ("old age, exhaustion, dusk, autumn", *Faiths & Avatars*), which came down
from Jergal with the rest when Bane, Bhaal and Myrkul took his power. Irildë gives the Heir three
attributes. Two of them, dusk and the eyes that drink fate, are Jergal's in canon; the third, the
breath, is what the third voice near Hedrack says it will take. The Black
Cyst was grooming Lareth "as the future Heir of Dusk" [S Sun 1:13], and the Candlekeep letter has
Hedrack as Jergal's exarch before his fall (CK). [I] Read together: **a nameless former servant
of the god of endings, raising an heir to the endings.** The Temple doesn't want a god; it wants
the office. Whoever holds the scroll decides when the last name is spoken. **Shareable as a
question** (whose dusk?); **silent** as the full plan.

[C] Recorded, not argued: Myrkul's Bedine name in Anauroch is **N'asr** (*Faiths & Avatars*), and
Malygris sleeps in Anauroch.

## 2. The Apocalypse is the Temple's plan, written as scripture — *strong*

APO itself says the fragments were forbidden "from the moment they were linked to the Temple and
Nebiros" [T].

- **Seals 1–4 are the four nodes.** Khelben's text reads "power over the fourth part of the World"
  as possibly the four basic Elements, and seal 6 ushers in cataclysms "with the diffusion of the
  Elements" (APO). Four Wards bind four elemental powers, and someone is waking "the 4 Elemental
  Nodes" tied to "the known ancient conflict" (WAR). The Chosen opens the first four seals, and
  "the Chosen differs by side" (APO); the Temple's Champion is "the mortal means meant to bridge
  Nebiros's prison" [S Sun 0:59]. [I] The Champion is the dark side's Chosen, and the Wards are the
  dwarves' seals over the first four.
- **There was a first Reckoning, and Moradin's forge ended it.** A "2nd Reckoning" (APO) needs a
  first. Khelben's dwarven chronicle has one: a world that trembled before four rising elemental
  powers, "unbound, unbalanced", and Moradin's answer (WAR). The Wards were forged from obsidian
  "mined from the burned ruins of the Temple" (WAR), so the first Reckoning ended with the Temple in
  ashes and its own stone turned against it. Even then Moradin says "The Chain gnaws **again**"
  (WAR).
- **The Red Horseman is fire.** His horse is "fiery" red, the colour of "the fire and blood of war"
  (APO). Flame's Bane was the fire Ward, forged when "The Fire Node stirs" (WAR). [I] If the
  horsemen are the nodes, the Red one is the Fire Node.

**Shareable**, without naming any module: the characters heard every piece.

**Silent backing**: in RttToEE (deep dive §3.12) the Champion summons the four Princes of
Elemental Evil into their four nodes. Four horsemen, four princes.

## 3. The White Horseman has already ridden — *medium*

"And I saw and behold a white horse: and he that sat on him had a bow; **and a crown was given unto
him**: and he went forth conquering" (APO). Most read him as disease and pestilence (APO).

- Bhaal came back "assembled", from pieces, rituals and ambition, "a few years ago" (AUC). A crown
  **given**: "This was… arranged." (AUC)
- "Murder does not spread. It does not fester. And yet now… it does." (AUC) Murder carrying a
  disease, and Talona "very close to the answer" (AUC). Pestilence, crowned.

[I] The first seal may have broken with Bhaal's return, and nobody counted it because nobody was
counting. This competes with §2's elemental reading of the first four seals. APO allows both: every
horseman there has more than one reading. **Shareable**, as Ancelm's reading of two handouts side by
side.

## 4. Whoever opens the seals is whoever turns the storm — *strong as a reading*

- "Behold **the Hand of God** who takes away the sins of the world", a divine scroll sealed with
  seven seals. The Hand of God is "ο Εκλεκτός", the Chosen (APO), who opens the first four.
- "A **hand unclean**, yet veiled by mercy's daughter" (MAL): Red Handed.
- "And only **the hand that suffers willingly**, / And only the blade that bleeds for another, / May
  turn aside the storm." (MAL)
- [C] Ilmater's symbol: "a pair of white human hands bound at the wrists with blood-red cord"
  (*Faiths & Avatars*). And APO gives the four horsemen an Ilmatari tradition.
- Thalassar: one person, perhaps, "will stand before the seals and decide" (APO). Khelben: seals are
  broken not by the wicked but by love, and rarely by the desperate [S Sat 11:08].

[I] Three hands, one office. The one who could open the seals and the one who could turn the storm
aside have the same profile, someone who suffers willingly and out of love, and only the choice in
front of the seals tells them apart. That is Thalassar's question and Aemund's gap, and it makes
Khelben's "They are a mirror" literal. **Shareable**: the best Ancelm theory in the pack.

**Silent backing**: the DM's line is John 1:29 with *Lamb* changed to *Hand*. In Revelation 5 only
the Lamb may open the seven seals, "for thou wast slain". The willing sufferer is the one who opens.

## 5. The fifth seal runs on counterfeit martyrdom — *medium*

- Seal 5 releases "τις μαρτυρικές κραυγές" of the world toward the gods, "(τρομακτικά ποσά ενέργειας
  δηλαδή)" (APO). In Greek a μάρτυρας is a martyr and a **witness**.
- Selimbrar's Seven Signs (SEL leaf 3): true martyrdom is willing (1), knowing (2), seeks no
  audience (3), **has measure (4)**, **stops (5)**, leaves someone else lighter (6), and **asks no
  recognition, "ούτε καν από τον θεό" (7)**.
- A cry to the gods asks to be recognised, which fails 7. Pain without measure and without end fails
  4 and 5, the two Pylacheus took out of his Handbook (SEL leaf 3).

[I] The fifth seal's fuel is exactly what Selimbrar called counterfeit: endless pain crying out to be
seen. A Handbook with rules 4 and 5 cut is a manual for making it, and the copy that "should not
exist" is in the hands of "the one who holds it" (SEL). The abbey's numbered blood vials and
Corvus's hunt for the bloodlines of Selimbrar's martyrs (OGH) fit a harvest. And because a μάρτυρας
is a witness, the people who remember are fuel too: the Living Testament, and "That which is
witnessed is not yet lost" [S Sat 1:45]. Ancelm's calling is the thing being burned. **Shareable**:
it's SEL read against APO, plus one Greek word.

**Silent backing**: Revelation 6:9, the fifth seal, "under the altar the souls of them that were
slain for the word of God, and for the testimony which they held", crying for their blood to be
avenged. The abbey's altar was five-sided [S Fri].

## 6. Why the Temple is early: us — *medium*

The deep dive (§3.5) has the Temple about 240 years ahead of its cycle. Two of the signs before it
were the party's own doing:

- [P] The Long Night happened *"when you went to the Demiplane of Shadow, to find Matt's memories"*
  [S Sun 0:16]. That's the 2014–15 party.
- [T] The rain of bodies followed Birakar and Temag's find at Karran-Kul "immediately", and Ancelm
  read "The Last Seer's Dream" as saying their actions "directly influenced Bhaal's return—or the
  prevention of it" (OGH).

[I] Darkness after the Shadow trip, Bhaal's return after Karran-Kul, then the Blood Moon, then the
nodes stirring. If someone "arranged" Bhaal's return (AUC), heroes were the tool, and the heroes were
us. **Shareable as a question**: "Did we do this?" It's the question a DM hopes to be asked.

## 7. "When red flame gutters but does not die" — *weak to medium*

The second line of Alaundo's prophecy. The DM glossed six of its lines (the bled moon, the first
crown, the dragon, the unclean hand, the god's wound, the Balance) and not this one (MAL).

[I] Two readings. **Bhaal**: red, killed, and back. Or **Flame's Bane**: the fire Ward, failing but
holding, its "last memory" still warm in Temag's palm (WAR), while the Fire Node stirs. "Then shall
the Balance falter, yet not break" has the same shape. If it's the Ward, Alaundo's Malygris prophecy
and Khelben's dwarven quest are one prophecy. **Shareable as a question.**

## 8. The order to forget — *medium*

- The Divine Order keeps parts of the Apocalypse from mortals "στα βάθη της Λήθης" (APO), in the
  depths of Lethe.
- Thaelith's new reading of Ruan Famkul: "that which **must be made** forgotten" (THA). An
  instruction, not a description.
- The Wards' making was "sealed", not erased (WAR). Moradin: "Hide the glyph." (WAR)

[I] Three orders to forget, from the gods and from a dwarven god. Dahast's mark may be one of them,
carried out by the dwarves at Delzoun's end and signed on the door they closed. **Shareable**, for
Dahast.

**Silent**: [C] Shar's portfolio includes **forgetfulness** and unrevealed secrets (*Faiths &
Avatars*; FRCS 3e). In Netheril her touch "could bring brief forgetfulness and solace or total loss
of memory", she gave each priest a secret name, and Jergal courted her (*Netheril: Empire of
Magic*). If something below the gods is driving the forgetting, the Lady of Loss is the obvious
suspect. A watch-for, not a theory.

## 9. Everything that mattered was said in the passive voice — *weak as plot, strong as character*

"This was… arranged." (AUC) "That which has been deliberately removed from remembrance." (ELF) The
Wards' making "has been sealed" (WAR). "THE DEAD HAVE BEEN READ." (WGV) "The final component was
secured." [S Sat 6:56] "A crown was given unto him." (APO) Every one of them hides who did it.

[T] It's Ancelm's own order: the **Children of the Passive Voice**, named for its doctrine, "Do not
alter history merely because you dislike it. Record what happened." (OGH) That answers NOTES §7
q11. [I] The Redactors learned the same grammar: a sentence with no subject is one nobody can be
blamed for. Ancelm's case, this whole campaign, is a grammar problem: find the subject of the
sentence. **Shareable.**

## 10. The sky has already shown the sixth seal — *silent*

Revelation 6:12–14, the sixth seal: "the sun became black as sackcloth of hair, and the moon became
as blood; And the stars of heaven fell unto the earth… And the heaven departed as a scroll". The
campaign has:

- the **Long Night**, almost two years of unnatural darkness (ELF);
- **a sky without stars**, in the Garden beneath Myth Drannor, inside the Long Night (ELF);
- the **Blood Moon**, 1160 (MAL);
- Irildë: "When the Moon bleeds and the sky weeps stars, The world shall be cloaked in the final
  veil." (OGH)

[I] If Panagiotis is following that chapter, the sixth seal's signs are behind us, and seals 1–5 are
already open (§3, §5). The elf stood under the sixth seal's sky holding a seed meant for "the sky
without stars", and didn't plant it.

And APO's own aside: this end, "(εντός ή εκτός εισαγωγικών και πάλι, ίσως και αρχής)", and the
White Horseman read as "the so-called 2nd Chance of the World" (APO). A **white** seed, from the
Keepers of First Dawn, who keep the memory that something existed before the elves (ELF). [I] The
seed may be the beginning the end is for.

**Silent**: it reads his source and guesses his clock. The in-world way in is a question for
Khelben, now in Reply 2: does the tome describe the sixth seal in full?

## 12. Small things, mostly silent

- **Thalassar.** θάλασσα, the sea. And the Red Horseman's second quote, "From the eternal sea he
  rises, creating armies on either shore, turning man against his brother, until man exists no
  more" (APO), is from the 1976 film *The Omen*, not Revelation. A man of the sea, whose name
  "brings something closer" when spoken. **Silent**: name-reading.
- **"Δεν είναι απλά relics. Είναι λύσεις."** (WAR) A λύση is a solution, and a loosing: "to loose
  the seals" in Revelation 5 is λῦσαι τὰς σφραγῖδας. A Ward of Binding named, in Greek, for undoing
  a seal. Maybe a pun. **Silent.**
- **The seventh seal's "7 aspects???"** *Weak.* There are six types of Bhaal's shard (BHA); the
  wound hidden in Malygris would make seven. **Silent.**
- **The Black Horseman's scales.** *Weak.* A rider who rations the bread and spares "the oil and the
  wine" (APO), beside Alaundo's "Balance" that falters (MAL) and a Compact with a loophole for the
  powerful (AUC). Thin.
- **"God, not Gods"** stays an ask (deep dive §8, item 9). For us, the candidates: Ao, "the one who
  wrote the rules"; Ilmater, the bound hands; Nebiros claiming to be the One; or something older
  than the pantheon, since no church has ever claimed the text and it may not come from this world
  at all (APO). The last fits §10 best.

# The bladesinger (primary character)

High elf, born **930 DR** in the Forest of Mir, **230** years old in 1160. (The draft
calls 930 "the Year of Doom"; in canon that name belongs to 714, when Myth Drannor
fell. See Questions #23.) Last of
**House Dahast**. No name. A mark nobody can read, a Light he can't command, a Song he
can only half remember, and a sealed letter for Khelben Arunsun.

Source: Panagiotis's `ELF BACKGROUND - Chris Draft to be finished`. The title says the
draft is mine to finish; this file is the finishing pass. Everything marked
**[proposed]** is my addition and needs Panagiotis's agreement.

---

## 1. Who he is, in five lines

- **He moves.** Orendyl's first lesson: "you have not yet learned to stop." Fear doesn't
  freeze him, it turns into rhythm. That is the bladesong.
- **He was raised by a whole community, not a family.** "Many hands. Many voices." He
  belongs to Sarenestar Ael'Vala more than to any bloodline, which is why being the
  last of a famous house means little to him and much to everyone else.
- **He has learned that doors open for him, and he doesn't trust it.** "You simply kept
  finding doors that were already open." No god has spoken. Every coincidence feels like
  a hand on his back.
- **He was taught that magic is a conversation, not a command.** Thaelith: "The Art is
  not command. The Art is conversation." Orendyl: the sword "was a language." He listens
  first.
- **He carries a debt he can't read.** He answered a question he doesn't remember, and
  the woman said "Good." He has spent every day since wondering what he agreed to.

## 2. How he plays at the table

**[proposed]**, built from the text:

- **Speech.** Quiet, and comfortable with silence ("silence could be as important as
  speech"). Asks short questions and lets the other person fill the gap: Thaelith's
  method.
- **The name.** See §3. Whatever he answers to, there's a beat before he says it.
- **Habit.** His feet are never still. He shifts his weight, paces, taps a rhythm. At
  rest he hums, and stops the moment he notices, because he's afraid of the third note.
- **In combat.** He doesn't ask "where should the sword go?" He asks "where should the
  world be when the sword arrives?" He positions and redirects rather than trading
  blows.
- **The mark.** Always covered. Saelith forbade copying it, and he still won't let
  anyone draw it.
- **Flaw.** He wants the answer to "Why me?" badly enough to walk into rooms he
  shouldn't. Thaelith worried he was "looking in the wrong direction".
- **Flaw.** He doesn't trust his own memory. After the fragments, every certainty comes
  with "I think".
- **What shakes him.** That he lowered his sword. "You simply remember that suddenly
  killing her seemed impossible." He doesn't know whether that was mercy, magic or
  something he chose, and the not-knowing is worse than any wound.

## 3. The name: a design choice, not a list

The text makes namelessness a plot point: "Something else had already written itself
where your name should have been." So the question isn't what to call him. It's **how
he handles being unnamed in the meantime.** Three ways, each with a different feel:

| Option | How it works | What it gives the story |
|---|---|---|
| **A. "Dahast"** | He answers to his house name alone. Outsiders take it for a personal name; he lets them | His mother loved single surviving names: "A single surviving name interested her more than a genealogy containing thousands." He *is* one. Every time someone says it, a dead house is spoken aloud. It also keeps "Dahast" in the open, which the woman reacted to. And the canon house arms are a **lap harp and a dagger**, a song and a blade |
| **B. The community's names** | "Little Star" to the elves of Mir; as an adult, one of the names they used: Little Dahast, Little Star, the Marked Child | Warm, but childish to his own ear. Good for a Mir elf to call him in a flashback |
| **C. A borrowed name** | He chooses a name on leaving Mir and treats it as a travelling cloak, to be put down when the true one comes | Lets him be introduced normally, but costs the mystery a little every time it's used |

**My recommendation: A.** It's the only option that comes straight from the text
(the house is his, the pendant is his, and his mother's whole life was about surviving
names). It's also the most playable: one short word, easy to say at a table, with a
story under it.

The **true name** stays hidden under the mark until the story releases it. That is
Panagiotis's call, and I'd rather not know in advance.

## 5. His background, in one order

The new sections that fill this order out are in **`Bladesinger - background additions.md`**,
written in Panagiotis's register and keyed to his section headings.

The draft has two endings. "THE DECISION" (he leaves Mir) comes before "The Song of the
Forgotten Light", whose events must happen before he leaves. This is the order I'd
propose (Questions #22). Section names are Panagiotis's.

1. **930 DR.** Elyndra Dahast flees "the burning city", reaches the hidden community
   in the Forest of Mir wounded, gives birth and dies. *The Child.* "Then he has no
   name." *The Mark.*
2. **Childhood in Mir.** *The Forest of Mir*; the community *Sarenestar Ael'Vala*.
3. **~940.** *The First Sword* (Orendyl, a wooden sword at about ten).
4. **~946.** *The First Bladesong* (at about sixteen).
5. **Youth to adulthood.** *The Second Teacher* (Thaelith); *Ithil'Vaer*; *The Song
   Before the First Light*; *The Keepers of First Dawn* (Lethariel and the white seed);
   *The First Signs*.
6. **Before 1152.** *The Long Night*; the expedition to Myth Drannor; *The Light*;
   *The Garden Disappears*.
7. **1152 →.** *The Years of Searching*. The silver leaf's inscription is found and
   Thaelith translates it ("When the ember remembers itself, follow the Song").
8. **Later.** *The Other Mystery* (Ruan Famkul, Delzoun); *The Question of Your Name*.
9. **Thaelith's last years.** *The Final Lesson* ("Do not make that mistake");
   *Thaelith's Last Letter*; *The Blood Moon* vision; *The Three Questions*. Her
   warning: "If the ember ever wakes again, do not search for the Garden. Follow the
   Song."
10. **After Thaelith's death.** *The Three Notes* (the dreams), then *The First Stanza*.
11. **The search for the keeper.** *The Last Memory*, Fragments I–IX: the open door,
    the dead elf, the woman.
12. **Now.** *The Decision*. He leaves Mir with the letter, the leaf, the seed and the
    mark, and heads for Waterdeep and Khelben. *ΤΡΕΧΟΥΣΑ ΘΕΣΗ*.

**[proposed] What the draft doesn't cover yet**, for Panagiotis and me to fill:

- **The expedition** in step 6: who went, and who didn't come back (Questions #24).
- **Step 7's 1152 → years.** Where he went and what he found. A few named places would
  make him feel travelled: ruins he searched, scholars he questioned.
- **Between step 11 and step 12.** How long after the woman did he leave? Did he go
  back to Mir first?
- **The road from Mir to Waterdeep.** And how he ends up with the party, who are in

AD&D 2nd Edition. Page references are to the printed books: *Complete Book of Elves*
(CBoE), *Player's Handbook* (PHB), *Dungeon Master's Guide* (DMG), *Elves of Evermeet*
(EoE), *Cormanthyr*, *Wizards and Rogues of the Realms* (WRR), *Demihumans of the
Realms* (DotR).

### The kit: Bladesinger, which means fighter/mage

- **Bladesinger is a fighter/mage kit and nothing else** (CBoE pp. 82, 88; EoE p. 110;
  Cormanthyr p. 9). There is no single-class route. War Wizard is also fighter/mage only
  (CBoE pp. 88–91).
- **The third option was the Spellfilcher**, not a "Spellsinger". It's a **mage/thief**
  kit in the CBoE (ch. 10), so a different class combination altogether. (A 2e
  Spellsinger does exist, but only as a human or half-elf wizard variant in WRR
  pp. 6–12.)
- **War Wizard** is a poor fit for him. It's the officer in charge of an elf town's
  defence, with a uniform, a code and a month a year with the unit (CBoE pp. 90–91). He
  is a wanderer with no unit.

**Decided (2026-10-08): fighter/mage, Bladesinger kit.** It's what the background
describes, and Orendyl was one.

### Bladesinger at a glance (CBoE pp. 88–90)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Minimums** | Str 13, Con 13, Dex 15, Int 15. "No Exceptions!" (p. 82) |
| **Bonus proficiencies** | Blind-fighting, Dancing |
| **Recommended** | Etiquette, Juggling, Singing, Tumbling, Weaponsmithing; weapon styles: one-weapon (fighting while casting), two-handed (both hands on the same blade, once spells run out) |
| **Weapon of choice** | +1 to hit and +1 damage, on top of the elf's +1 to hit with swords. The ceiling: multi-class characters can't specialise (p. 82) |
| **Casting in melee** | No attack in a round he casts, but +(level ÷ 2) + 1 AC against melee (not rear, not missiles). One-handed casting at +2 casting time. Which level to use for a fighter/mage isn't stated: **DM call** |
| **Maneuvers** | +1 per 4 levels to offset the penalties for disarming and similar |
| **Tattoo** | The guild animal for his weapon (long sword → great cat) |
| **Hindrance: one weapon** | One weapon for life. −1 to hit with all others, even when proficient. Loses the elven +1 with bows |
| **Hindrance: armour** | Nothing heavier than elven chain or studded leather. And a fighter/mage casts only "unarmored or clad in elvenmade or magical armor" (p. 88), so **elven chain** is the only armour that lets him cast. It "can never be purchased" (PHB p. 45) |
| **Hindrance: duty** | Must aid any elf in need, at risk of life and limb, unless that elf is a proven enemy |
| **Wealth** | 3d6×10 gp; buys the ornate weapon, the rest goes to the guild |

Separate from the kit: the **bladesong fighting style** (CBoE pp. 70–72) is an
optional weapon style for warriors and warrior/mages. It costs 3 weapon slots and gives
+1 AC, or +1 to hit, or attack-and-parry, switchable each round, with no shield. Worth
asking whether Panagiotis allows it **on top of** the kit.

### Elf race facts (PHB pp. 20–22; CBoE pp. 76, 81)

- High elf: **+1 Dex, −1 Con**. Infravision 60 ft. 90% resistance to *sleep* and
  *charm*. +1 to hit with long and short swords and with bows. Finds secret doors.
- **Sun elf, and the grey-elf question.** House Dahast is a **gold-elf** house
  (*Cormanthyr* p. 112), so by his mother he's a sun elf. Two 2e books pull different
  ways:
  - **CBoE p. 25:** the Realms' gold elves "do not closely resemble the standard high
    elves … seem more like grey elves" (the moon elves are the ones like CBoE high
    elves). On that reading he takes the **CBoE grey-elf package** (p. 80): **+2 Int,
    +1 Dex, −1 Str, −2 Con**; Str max 17, Con max 16, Int max 20; −3 reactions with
    non-elves, −1 with other elves; **+15% XP**.
  - **EoE p. 104:** gold elves *look* like CBoE grey elves but keep the standard **+1
    Dex / −1 Con** (plus +1 to riding checks).
  - So it's Panagiotis's ruling which package applies (Questions #8). His father is
    unknown, and the silver motifs (the leaf, the silver trees, "Little Star") lean moon:
    a sun-elf house name on a boy drawn to silver.
- **Level limits** (DMG p. 15): fighter **12**, mage **15**. The optional ways past
  them (DMG Table 8; CBoE p. 69 double XP) are Panagiotis's choice. DotR p. 9 says
  multi-class characters can't use the exceeding-limits rule at all.

### The age problem: 230 is middle age (superseded: Panagiotis applies no middle age)

Both the PHB (Middle Age 175, Old 233) and the CBoE (high elf Middle Age 175–249) put
230 in **middle age: −1 Str, −1 Con, +1 Int, +1 Wis**. And Cormanthyr (p. 9) says elves
born after about 300 DR "age as standard AD&D game elves", so the longer elven aging
doesn't save him.

What he must roll to meet the kit after race and age adjustments, under each package:

| Ability | Kit minimum | Standard (+1 Dex, −1 Con) + age | Needs | Grey-elf package (+2 Int, +1 Dex, −1 Str, −2 Con) + age | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Str | 13 | −1 (age) | **14** | −1 (race), −1 (age) | **15** |
| Con | 13 | −1 (race), −1 (age) | **15** | −2 (race), −1 (age) | **16** |
| Dex | 15 | +1 (race) | **14** | +1 (race) | **14** |
| Int | 15 | +1 (age) | **14** | +2 (race), +1 (age) | **12** |

The grey-elf package makes him a better mage and a frailer swordsman, which suits a
scholar's son. It also costs 15% more XP on top of the fighter/mage split.

On the bright side, CBoE p. 70 (optional) gives **one extra non-weapon proficiency
per 10 years over 100**: **+13** at 230 (no adventuring ones such as blind-fighting).
That is a lot of lore, music, languages and crafts for a 230-year-old.

**Question for Panagiotis:** apply middle age, keep the age and waive it, or make him
younger? (If the age matters to the story, keeping 930 and applying the modifiers
is the honest version.)

### [proposed] Spellbook flavour

Thaelith taught him that magic is a conversation, and the kit expects him to carry
*detect magic* for his duty to elves. For a starting book: *detect magic*, *read magic*,
*comprehend languages*, *identify*, *shield*, plus one or two that suit a duellist
(*magic missile*, *feather fall* or *jump*). He chooses later spells for listening and
moving, not for big destruction.

## 7. Where he came from, in canon and in the old campaign

- **House Dahast is canon** (*Cormanthyr* pp. 72, 112–113). It was one of the twelve
  First Houses of Cormanthyr, gold elves, with arms of **a lap harp and a dagger** on
  white and midnight blue: music and a blade, a fair description of a bladesinger. Alea
  Dahast, High Lady of Art and Spell-Major of the Akh'Faer, died in Flamerule 714 in one
  of the last battles of the Weeping War. The house was "nearly extinct" before the
  fall; its last named members are the patriarch **Purtham** (Alea's grandson) and the
  clan heir **Lhoris**, who secretly murdered his elder brothers. How Elyndra descends
  from them is open (Questions #40).
- **Khelben knew that world.** Born in 414, he left Myth Drannor in 449 DR, and has
  been in Waterdeep since **1150** (GHotR p. 122). If anyone alive can tell a Dahast
  orphan about his house, it's him. In 1160 his home is **Arunsun Tower**, not yet
  Blackstaff Tower.
- **"Sarenestar" is canon:** the elves of Tethir call the Forest of Mir by that name,
  and nobody knows "whether this word refers to the forest or the elves who once dwelt
  there" (*Lands of Intrigue: Tethyr* p. 53). Panagiotis's community name, *Sarenestar
  Ael'Vala*, sits right on that ambiguity. Canon Mir also hides **Myth Dyraalis**, a
  mythal-town of elves, gnomes and escaped slaves founded in −375 DR (p. 91): a model
  for a community that survives "because we learned not to be found".
- **The Forest of Mir** was a 2014 location. Sylvia's briefing (sent 25 September 2014)
  describes over 80,000 elves in 8–10 tribes, with underground dwellings that may
  connect "with a vast underground network beneath all the Realms", and very little to
  do with outsiders. The draft's hidden community of about 200 ("We survived because we
  learned not to be found") fits as one small group *within* that, not a tribe of its
  own. **[proposed]**
- **Salendra**, first of the Nine, was an elven archmage "from the place today known
  as Evereska". If Panagiotis wants an elven bloodline back to the Legion, House Dahast
  is the obvious candidate. That is his call. See `Campaign web.md` §3 and §8.

## 8. With the Oghmanite

The two backgrounds were written to meet. The full table is in `Campaign web.md` §1.
The short version:

- The Oghmanite was told: "**Find the reader.** Find the one whom the light remembers
  at dusk. And when you meet him… **ask him what he remembers.**" The elf carries the
  Light and has gaps in his memory.
- The elf was told: "**Seek the one who remembers.**" The Oghmanite's mentor believed
  he carries part of the Living Testament.
- The Oghmanite's *Dispel Falsehood* works against "spells meant to erase/alter memory
  (like the *Forget* spell)". The elf's Fragments I–IX look like exactly that.
- The same woman said "Not yet" to both of them.

**[proposed] How they meet.** The elf is bound for Waterdeep; the Oghmanite and
Birakar are in Westgate, which isn't on the road from Mir. Two ways to bring them
together:

1. **Through Khelben.** Thaelith's letter reaches Khelben, and Khelben sends the elf
   on to the Harpers in Westgate, where the Oghmanite is.
2. **Through the dreams.** The Teziir dreamer lives on the Dragon Coast, like Westgate.
   If the elf hears of a girl who dreams of "a man bound … under chains that never
   break", it echoes his own vision closely enough to pull him east.

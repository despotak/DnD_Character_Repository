BACKGROUND – ELF

**Race:** High Elf
**Family Origins:** Myth Drannor
**Birth:** 930 DR, the Year of Doom
**Age in 1160 DR:** 230 years
**Raised:** Forest of Mir
**True name:** Unknown — even to himself
**House:** Dahast
**Possible kits:**

* **Bladesinger** — primary recommendation for the story
* **War Wizard** — alternative
* **Spellsinger** — if this is the kit you mean; strictly speaking, Spellsinger is not listed among the Complete Book of Elves kits, while Bladesinger and War Wizard are.

There are names that are inherited.

There are names that are earned.

There are names given by parents, by kings, by teachers, by gods.

And there are those who grow into adulthood before realizing that they were never given one at all.

You are one of those.

You were born in the shadow of a dead city.

Your mother came from **House Dahast**, one of the ancient noble houses of Cormanthyr and one of the families whose history was woven deeply into Myth Drannor. The Dahasts had produced scholars, warriors, mages and High Magic practitioners. Among their most celebrated ancestors was **Alea Dahast**, the High Lady of Art, who helped raise the mythal of Myth Drannor and later became Spell-Major of the Akh'Faer.

By the time of your birth, however, almost nothing remained of that glory. Myth Drannor had fallen centuries before. The great houses had scattered. The old city had become a ruin. And House Dahast, which had once possessed a villa in the heart of the Old City, had dwindled almost to nothing.

Your mother was one of its last surviving member. She would not live long enough to tell you any of this.

Your mother was called **Elyndra Dahast**. She was a scholar. Elyndra spent much of her life studying things that other elves considered too old, too obscure, or too insignificant to matter.

Old songs.

Fragmentary genealogies.

Forgotten magical traditions.

Ancient ruins.

The movements and migrations of elven peoples.

The relationship between swordsmanship and magic.

The strange places where one culture's history crossed another's.

She had a particular fascination with things that survived after their civilizations had vanished. A broken inscription interested her more than a perfect monument. A half-remembered song interested her more than a famous one. A single surviving name interested her more than a genealogy containing thousands. Perhaps this was because she knew, even before she understood why, that her own family was disappearing.

House Dahast had survived the Weeping War only as a shadow of what it had once been. The family name remained. The memories remained. But the people were gone. One by one, they had died. One by one, their stories had become fragments. Until there was only Elyndra.

And then there was you.

**THE FALLING STAR**: The Weeping War had happened centuries earlier, but Myth Drannor had never truly become a dead place. Its ruins retained magic, memory and danger. Even centuries later, the remnants of its mythal and the strange magical forces surrounding the ruins could make the city feel less like a corpse than a sleeping thing.

Elyndra had fled the burning city. She went east and south, away from the ruins. She traveled toward the **Forest of Mir**. She carried almost nothing. A small silver pendant bearing the old Dahast emblem. A few personal papers. One fragment of an old song.

She reached the hidden elven community where she hoped to find shelter. She was exhausted. She was wounded. The elves there tried to save her. They did.

She gave birth shortly before she died. You survived. Your mother did not.

**THE CHILD:** The elves gathered around the newborn child. An elder named **Saelith Moonbrook** was the first to hold you. She looked at you. Then at the dead woman. Then at the silver Dahast pendant.

Someone eventually asked the question that should have had the simplest answer. "What is his name?"

There was no answer. Your mother had not told anyone. No written name was found. No family name had been prepared for you. No final instruction was given.

Only the pendant. Only the old song. And you.

Saelith asked again. Nothing. At last, she said: "Then he has no name."

No one argued. No one invented one. That decision would follow you for your entire life.

You were simply **the child**. Sometimes **Little Dahast**. Sometimes **Little Star**. Sometimes, particularly as you grew older, **the Marked Child**.

But never by a true name.

The elves of the Forest of Mir did not regard this as a tragedy. At first.

Elven children were often given names surrounded by ceremony, family memory and expectation. You had none of those things. Instead, the community decided that you would simply grow. Perhaps one day a name would come. Perhaps you would choose one. Perhaps your family name would eventually be enough.

No one knew that the absence of your name would become one of the deepest mysteries of your life.

**THE MARK**: There was one other thing. The mark. It had been on your body from the moment you were born.

A dark symbol.

The elves examined it carefully. No ordinary spell revealed anything. It did not respond to detect magic. It did not glow beneath moonlight. It did not change when touched by silver. It did not behave like an elven rune.

Saelith eventually forbade anyone from cutting, copying, painting or magically reproducing it.

When asked what it meant, she answered: "It is a mark."

Someone asked: "A mark of what?"

Saelith looked at the child. Then at the symbol. "That is not the same question."

And finally: "It is a mark. Not an answer."

So you grew up with it.

**THE FOREST OF MIR:** Your childhood was not unhappy. You were not raised as an orphan in misery. You were raised by elves. You knew trees before you knew cities. You knew the language of birds before you understood politics. You learned the phases of the moon. You learned which streams were safe to drink from. You learned the names of flowers. You learned which ruins should be left untouched. You learned that ancient things were not necessarily dead things. And you learned that silence could be as important as speech.

The community became your family. There was no single father or mother. There were many hands. Many voices. Many teachers. One of them became particularly important.

His name was **Orendyl Greenleaf**. He was old even by elven standards. And he was a bladesinger.

**THE FIRST SWORD:** To Orendyl your family name or anything else, did not matter. He certainly did not care about the strange circumstances of your birth. He saw a child who moved strangely.

You were always moving. When you ran, you changed direction without thinking. When you climbed trees, you seemed to anticipate branches before you saw them. When you played, your movements became rhythmic.

So Orendyl gave you a wooden sword. You were perhaps ten years old. You immediately swung it too hard. Orendyl knocked it from your hand.

You picked it up. He knocked it away again. You picked it up a third time. He smiled. "Good."

You asked why.

"Because you have not yet learned to stop."

Your training began. Orendyl taught you that the sword was not primarily a weapon. It was a language. Your feet learned rhythm. Your hands learned precision. Your breathing became measured. Your movements became deliberate.

And eventually, magic entered the lessons. Through movement. Orendyl told you: "You are not learning to fight."

You frowned. "Then what am I learning?"

He answered: "To move."

Years later, you would understand the deeper lesson. A fighter asks where the sword should go.

A bladesinger asks: **Where should the world be when the sword arrives?**

**THE FIRST BLADESONG:** You were perhaps sixteen when it happened. A predator entered the settlement. Not something extraordinary. Not a monster from legend. Just something dangerous enough that the adults needed to respond quickly. You found yourself between it and another child.

You drew your sword. You were afraid. You remember that clearly. Your hands shook. Your heart raced. Then something changed.

You breathed. Once. Twice.

The fear did not disappear. Instead, it became rhythm. Your feet moved. Your sword followed. You began to turn. Not wildly. Not desperately. Perfectly.

For a few seconds, everything seemed to become part of the same movement. Your breathing. Your heartbeat. The sword. The forest. The creature. The space between you. The magic.

The first **Bladesong** came without instruction. When it ended, Orendyl simply stood there. You got no congratulation nor any praise from him. He only said: "Now you have begun."

**THE SECOND TEACHER:** There was another person who would shape your life. Her name was **Thaelith Moonwhisper**. She was a scholar, mage and keeper of ancient elven knowledge. She was also one of the few elves who did not regard your birth as a simple tragedy.

Thaelith asked questions. Too many questions.

One of those bothered you too much. “Why had the child been born with a mark that appeared nowhere in the Dahast records?”

Thaelith searched. She searched elven genealogies. Old Cormanthyr texts. Fragments of Myth Drannor's archives. Family records. Songs. Ruins. She found nothing.

Eventually, however, she began teaching you.

Thaelith taught you that magic was not simply the act of imposing your will upon reality.

She told you: "The Art is not command."

Then: "The Art is conversation."

You studied ancient elven magical traditions. You studied forgotten songs. You learned how elves once understood magic before the modern traditions had divided it into schools, formulas and disciplines.

You learned that some ancient elves spoke of a force they called **Ithil'Vaer**.

The First Light.

Something older than moonlight or divine radiance.

A creative impulse. The first act by which possibility became reality.

Thaelith showed you a fragment of an ancient text:

Before the first moon, before the first star,
before the first leaf knew green,
or flame had known the warmth of flame,
there was Light.

And the Light sang.

She never told you what the passage meant. Instead, she taught you to listen.

**THE SONG BEFORE THE FIRST LIGHT:** Eventually Thaelith introduced you to an ancient elven song.

**Ithil'Vaer en'Lath.** The Song Before the First Light.

The oldest traditions said it was to be sung beneath an open sky, rarely, and never loudly. The first verse spoke of remembering the Song before the singer. The second spoke of a star that had no sky. The third spoke of a Garden where beginnings slept.

And finally: For the Light cannot be summoned. It can only be given room to return.

You did not understand.

Not then.

You would spend years trying.

**THE KEEPERS OF FIRST DAWN:** Thaelith eventually told you of an ancient elven order called the **Keepers of First Dawn**, or **Vael'Shaerith**.

She said they were guardians of a memory. The memory that something existed before the civilizations of elves. Before their kingdoms. Before their wars. Before their songs. Before even the stars had been given their names.

The order had nearly vanished. One of the last Keepers you encountered was an ancient elf named **Lethariel Starbough**.

He studied you for a long time. Then he gave you a small white seed. It looked dead.

He said: "When you see the sky without stars, plant this where you find the answer."

You asked what answer.

He smiled. "That is why you must find it."

You kept the seed.

**THE FIRST SIGNS:** Your life continued. Sword. Magic. Song. Study. Exploration.

And sometimes, strange things happened.

A crescent shape appeared repeatedly in places where it should not have been.

A ruin that should have been dangerous somehow became safe when you followed a particular path.

An ancient symbol of **Corellon** appeared beneath a fallen tree in the Silent Wood, beside a broken sword.

Once, in an abandoned sanctuary, a statue of Corellon seemed to point toward a hidden passage.

You never heard a divine voice. No god appeared before you. No prophecy announced your destiny. You simply kept finding doors that were already open. Or paths that existed only because you happened to look in the right direction. You never knew whether these were coincidences.

Thaelith never told you. If she suspected something, she kept it to herself.

**THE LONG NIGHT:** Then came the Long Night. For almost two years, Faerûn endured an unnatural darkness. And during that darkness, you returned to the outskirts of Myth Drannor.

The darkness seemed to awaken things that had slept beneath the city. You saw a single star. There should have been none. Yet it hung above the ruins.

You followed it. You entered places that should have been inaccessible.

You found old passages. Old sanctuaries. Old memories. Eventually, you found something impossible.

A garden.

The garden did not belong beneath Myth Drannor. It did not belong anywhere.

White trees stood beneath a sky that had no stars. Silver grass moved without wind. Flowers opened and closed as though remembering sunlight. At the center stood a still pool. And within that pool was a seed.

A thing that looked like a point of sunrise trapped inside something smaller than a human hand. You knew immediately that it was the thing Lethariel had spoken about.

The Seed.

You touched it.

And the world disappeared.

**THE LIGHT:** You saw darkness.

Then a point. A single point of Light. From it came Song. From Song came movement. From movement came form. From form came color. From color came life. You saw stars born. You saw worlds begin. You saw magic spread through creation. You saw life emerge. Then you saw the Light fracture.

One fragment fell away. Into darkness. You saw chains. You saw a black wound. And within that darkness stood a figure.

You had no idea what you were seeing. But something about the figure disturbed you more deeply than any monster ever had.

The figure was not destroying creation. He was refusing its ending.

Then you heard a voice. Its was ancient. You had never met her. Yet you knew who she was.

She said: "Do not fear him."

Then: "He forgot what it means to end."

And finally: "You are not here."

You looked toward her. She touched your chest. "You are carrying it."

The Light entered you.

**THE GARDEN DISAPPEARS:** When you awoke, the garden was gone. The Seed was gone. Most of the expedition was dead. Some had vanished. You survived.

And the Light was no longer outside you. It was within you.

You could not simply call upon it. You could not command it. You did not even fully understand what had happened.

But afterward, your dreams changed. Sometimes you dreamed of warmth. Sometimes of music. Sometimes of an enormous silence just before something was born. Sometimes you dreamed of a tall elven figure carrying a sword and a brush, drawing lines of light into empty space.

You never saw his face.

**THE YEARS OF SEARCHING:** From approximately **1152 DR onward**, your life became a long search. You traveled. You studied ruins. You sought ancient elven sanctuaries. You questioned scholars. You followed songs. You investigated fragments of the Keepers of First Dawn. You searched for references to the Garden.

And you kept searching for an answer to the greatest mystery of all: **Why you?**

The Light gave you no answer. Corellon gave you no answer. The ancient texts gave you no answer. And Thaelith increasingly became concerned that you were looking in the wrong direction, because she believed something else was waiting.

Something much older. Something that had been present before you ever began searching for the Light.

**THE OTHER MYSTERY:** It began with your birthmark. Thaelith had never stopped studying it. For decades she had searched elven records. Then, eventually, she found something.

Surprisingly, it was a dwarven record. The symbol appeared in a damaged fragment written in an ancient dwarven tongue.

The dwarves had a name for it: **RUAN FAMKUL**

The closest translation was: “*That which has been deliberately removed from remembrance.*”

But Thaelith believed that translation referred to something forgotten by design, rather than destroyed or erased.

Thaelith found the symbol in several fragmentary references. But one collection of records troubled her more than the others.

The final days of **Delzoun**.

There were references to an ancient northern route. A place where dwarven roads disappeared beneath mountains. And, strangely, references to elves.

Your mentor could not understand why.

Thaelith found several damaged passages.

One mentioned: "...where the anvils froze beneath the mountain..."

Another: "...the road into the valley was closed..."

Another: "... restore what was broken..."

And then a sentence that disturbed her more than all the rest: **"They came from the west, though no elf had crossed the passes."**

Thaelith read it again. And again. There should have been no elves there.

Not according to the known history of Delzoun. Not according to the surviving elven records. Yet the dwarven account insisted that someone had come from the west.

And beside the passage was the same symbol you had carried since birth.

**THE QUESTION OF YOUR NAME:** Thaelith eventually realized something else. Something she had never asked.

She asked you: "What was your name when you were born?"

You told her. You had no name. She stared at you.

Then she asked: "Your mother never gave you one?"

"No."

"Anyone else?"

"No."

"No one?"

You shook your head.

Thaelith became silent. Then she said: "I have spent years believing no one named you."

She looked at the mark. "That isn't true."

You asked what she meant.

She answered: "Your mother did not fail to name you."

A pause. "She couldn't."

You asked why. Thaelith touched the mark. "Something else had already written itself where your name should have been."

She did not know what that meant. And for once, the great scholar had no theory.

Only a warning.

**THE FINAL LESSON:** Thaelith grew old. Older than most people realized. She knew that she would not have many years left. Before her departure, she gave you several final lessons.

One evening she told you:

"There are mysteries that belong to the heart. There are mysteries that belong to the stars. And there are mysteries that belong beneath the earth."

She looked toward the north. "Yours may belong to all three."

You asked whether the mark was connected to the Light.

She immediately said: "No."

You were surprised.

She continued: "Do not make that mistake."

The answer was unusually firm. "The Light is one mystery. This is another. They may both have chosen you. That does not make them the same."

Those words would prove important.

**THAELITH'S LAST LETTER:** Thaelith wrote a letter. It was sealed. It was addressed to one person:

**Khelben Arunson.**

You did not know why. Thaelith had spoken of Khelben only rarely. She trusted him. That was enough.

She left it to you with the instructions to deliver it yourself to him.

**THE BLOOD MOON:** That night, something happened that had never happened before.

The mark changed. The black lines became darker. Sharper. For the first time, the symbol seemed almost three-dimensional.

You looked toward the moon. And suddenly you were somewhere else.

You saw mountains. Immense black mountains beneath a white sky. You saw snow. You saw a valley surrounded by stone. You saw enormous anvils covered in frost. You saw dwarves walking silently through snow. You saw a stone door closing.

And, finally—the symbol. The same symbol you’ve been carrying since birth. Carved into the door.

Then the vision ended.

You were standing beneath the Blood Moon. Your heart was pounding. The mark had returned to normal.

**THE THREE QUESTIONS:** For the first time, the pieces of your life seemed to form three separate paths.

The first path was the Light. The Garden. The Song. The Seed. The mysterious force of Light. The figure. The Black Wound.

The question of beginning and ending.

The second path was your blood. House Dahast. Myth Drannor. Your mother. Your missing name.

The unexplained circumstances of your birth.

And the third path was the mark. Ruan Famkul. Delzoun. The unknown elves. The frozen anvils. The sealed valley. The unfinished sentence: "...restore what was broken..."

You understood one thing clearly. They were not the same mystery.

But somehow all three mysteries had converged upon the same person.

You.

**THE DECISION:** You took the letter. You placed it beside the silver Dahast pendant your mother had carried from Myth Drannor.

You left the Forest of Mir. You left because your mentor had trusted someone. And because the dead sometimes leave behind questions that the living have a responsibility to ask.

You carried your sword. You carried the white seed. You carried the silver Dahast pendant. You carried Thaelith's letter. And beneath your clothes, against your skin, you carried the ancient mark.

You were going to find **Khelben Arunsun**.

And ask him to help you understand the mark you had carried since the day you were born.

**THE FOREST OF MIR COMMUNITY**: **Lethariel's Refuge**

The elves who live there call their home: **Sarenestar Ael'Vala**

roughly: **The Grove Beneath the Old Stars**

It is hidden deep in the Forest of Mir. The community is small—perhaps **two hundred elves** at the time of your birth.

They are descendants of several ancient elven peoples who remained in the region after the great forests changed.

Their philosophy is simple: **"We survived because we learned not to be found."**

**The Song of the Forgotten Light:** There came a time when the questions of your childhood ceased to be separate questions.

For years you had carried them as one carries stones in a pouch: the mark upon your skin, the absence of a name, the silver Dahast leaf left by your mother, the white seed given by the last Keeper, the strange ember that had awakened within you beneath the ruins of Myth Drannor.

You had thought them unrelated. Thaelith had taught you otherwise. They were not one mystery, but they were all pointing toward something older than yourself. Before she died, Thaelith had given you one final instruction. It was more of a warning.

**“If the ember ever wakes again, do not search for the Garden.”**

**“Follow the Song.”**

**<u>The Silver Leaf:</u>** Among the few possessions that had survived from your mother was a small silver leaf bearing the emblem of House Dahast. For most of your life you had carried it without knowing why.

After the Garden beneath Myth Drannor, when the ember had awakened inside you and the impossible Seed had vanished, you examined the leaf again. On its reverse, where the silver had been worn almost smooth by time, you discovered writing.

Ancient Elvish.

Only a few words remained legible. Thaelith translated them. She read them twice. Then a third time.

And finally she looked at you. **“When the ember remembers itself, follow the Song.”**

You asked her what Song. She did not answer.

Perhaps she did not know. Perhaps she had known for years and had been waiting for the right moment.

She only said: “Some knowledge is not written because writing can be destroyed. Some knowledge is given to those who can carry it. And some knowledge survives only because someone remembers.”

She touched the silver leaf.

“If you hear the Song, listen carefully. Do not ask where it leads. Ask what it remembers.”

Those were among her last lessons.

**<u>The Three Notes:</u>** After Thaelith's death, the dreams began. They came rarely. Never with warning. You would find yourself standing in a forest beneath a sky without stars. There was no moon.

No wind.

No birds.

Only silence.

Ahead stood a tall elven figure. You could never see the face.

Sometimes the figure held a sword. Sometimes a brush. Sometimes nothing at all.

The elf would raise one hand. A line of pale light would appear in the darkness. And then there would be music.

Only three notes. Always the same.

The first seemed to come from very far away.

The second seemed to come from beneath the earth.

The third seemed to come from within your own chest.

Then the figure would turn toward you. Not speaking. Only waiting.

Every time you tried to approach, you awoke. For years you dismissed the dreams. Until, one night, you realized something.

You knew those three notes. It was something you had heard when the ember first awakened.

And then you he remembered Thaelith's words… **Follow the Song.**

**<u>The First Stanza:</u>** The Song eventually came to you. It appeared in fragments within your memory, as though you were remembering something you had never learned.

The words formed slowly. And when they were complete, you wrote them down.

**<u>The First Stanza — The Song Before the Singer</u>**

Before the moon had learned to wander,
before the stars had learned their names,
before the leaf had known the green
or flame had known the warmth of flame,

there was a sound beneath the silence,
there was a breath before the breath.

And from the sound came first the Song,
and from the Song the living world.

**Remember.**

Not the song of kings.
Not the song of grief.
Not the song of those who came after.

**Remember the Song before the singer.**

For the Song was broken,
but not destroyed.

Its first note was given to memory.
Its second to a living voice.
Its third to stone.

And the last note waits
where no living elf remembers it.

**Seek not the place.**

**Seek the one who remembers.**

Find the last voice beneath the leaves of time,

the keeper of a melody
no living elf has heard
for four thousand years.

When the ancient voice remembers,

**listen.**

Do not ask what the Song means.

Ask what it has forgotten.

You read it many times. The meaning was not clear. But one line was.

Someone still remembered. Someone impossibly old. Someone who carried a melody no living elf had heard for four thousand years.

**<u>The Last Memory:</u> You** sought the ancient elf. The period of this seeking is unclear in your memory. You cannot explain why, but you have only fragments of this quest.

The first thing you remember clearly: A wooden door. It was already open. You remember thinking that this was strange. You had come expecting to knock. You remember stepping inside.

There was no sound. No conversation. No movement. Then you remember seeing the old elf. Sitting at his desk. Dead.

His eyes were open. A book was lying before him. One hand rested upon the page. The other hung beside the chair.

There was blood. Very little. Far too little…

Then the memory breaks.

<u>Fragment II:</u> A woman standing beside him. Tall. Pale. Dark hair. Something red upon her lips.

You remember thinking: **Beautiful.**

Then immediately: **Fear.**

You remember both thoughts at exactly the same time. You cannot remember which came first.

<u>Fragment III</u>: You remember her voice. “You are late.”

Nothing else.

Another fragment surfaces: “No. Don't.”

And then: “Not yet.”

You cannot determine which of the three she actually said.

<u>Fragment IV</u>: this fragment is most disturbing. You remember drawing your sword. You remember being furious.

You remember thinking: **She killed him.**

You remember wanting to kill her. You remember taking one step toward her. Your sword was raised.

And then— **you lowered it.**

You don't remember deciding to. You don't remember being forced. You simply remember that suddenly killing her seemed impossible.

**Impossible.**

Then: She looked at the sword. Then at you. And smiled….Almost sadly.

<u>Fragment V</u>: when you tried to remember the face, you remembered in heartbeats, someone standing beneath silver trees. There is music somewhere.

Your hear laughter. Perhaps she was. Perhaps someone else. You cannot remember.

<u>Fragment VI</u>: you remember her humming…Only three notes.

And those three notes are exactly the same three notes that occasionally appear in his dreams.

However, there is something wrong. Every time you tried to reproduce them, you can remember only two.

The third disappears.

You know there were three. You simply cannot remember the third.

<u>Fragment VII</u>: You remember her asking you something. You cannot remember the question, but you remember answering it.

You are not sure what your answer was.

You remember her smiling. You remember her saying: **“Good.”**

But you don't know what you agreed to.

<u>Fragment VIII</u>: You remember her fingers touching the silver leaf. Your mother's pendant.

She whispered: **“Dahast.”**

**In another fragment, you remember a whisper saying “She believed you would find the Song.”**

**You froze, asking if she knew your mother. After a long pause, she said “No. But I knew the woman who taught her mother.”**

In another fragment you remember walking outside. There is rain. She touches an ancient tree. She takes a leaf. Makes several tiny cuts.

Then says: **“An elf does not call another elf. He leaves something behind that can be remembered.”**

Then the memory breaks.

<u>FRAGMENT IX</u>: this fragment is almost dreamlike. You remember leaving. You remember walking through trees. The moon was enormous. There were silver leaves falling around you.

None touched the ground. Every leaf disappeared before it landed.

You remember hearing someone singing. You remember turning.

Nobody was there.

Then you looked back. Nothing.

Fragments or not, you have been desperate for answers. You decided to take your chances in Waterdeep and Khelben Arunson!

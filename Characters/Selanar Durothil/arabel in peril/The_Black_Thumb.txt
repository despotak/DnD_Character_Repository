The world dissolved into a dizzying vortex of azure and silver, the chaos of battle replaced by the cold, silent opulence of a gothic manor. Selanar stumbled, his equilibrium screaming in protest. He was in the DeWinter estate in Suzail, a place of legend whispered in fearful tones even in Myth Drannor. Before him stood Lady Olivia, her smile a predatory curve on her porcelain face.

The charm was not a crude hammer to his will, but a silken whisper that slid past his defenses. It wasn't a command, but an invitation to a truth his own nature as a bon viveur could not deny: the allure of raw, untamed power. He, the Seeker of Mysteries, was presented with the ultimate mystery—the precipice of undeath. Her words coiled around his mind, promising knowledge beyond the Weave, sensations beyond the flesh. He saw not a monster, but a dark muse.

*"Don't be afraid, my clever elf,"* she purred, her voice a melody that bypassed his ears and resonated directly in his soul. A wave of placid adoration washed over him, a magical compulsion that felt as natural as breathing. Fear? Why would he fear this magnificent creature? She was perfection, power, and safety all at once. The battle, the golem, Thalis, Drako… they were unimportant phantoms from another life.

She glided closer, her fingers tracing the line of his jaw. *"You fought so well. So much passion. So much life."* Her eyes, pools of ancient night, held his, and he felt his will dissolve like sugar in wine. *"You deserve more than a life of scrabbling in the dirt with those mortals. You deserve eternity."*

When her fangs descended, he did not resist. He tilted his head, offering his throat as a willing sacrifice. The bite was not pain, but a shocking, ecstatic release. He felt his vitality, his very essence, flowing out of him, a torrent drawn into the vortex of her being. His legs buckled, but she held him fast, her strength supernatural.

** **
Just as the world began to grey at the edges, she pulled back. Blood, his blood, jeweled her lips. Then she did something unexpected. With a manicured nail, she sliced open her own wrist. Dark, potent blood, impossibly rich, welled from the wound.

*"Drink,"* she commanded, her voice no longer a gentle melody but a law of nature. *"Drink, and be reborn."*

Charmed and enthralled, he obeyed. He latched onto her wrist, and the blood that filled his mouth was like nothing he had ever known. It was liquid power, ancient secrets, and the chilling promise of forever. The taste was of ancient dust, cold iron, and a power so profound it burned through him, rewriting his very being. The last thing he remembered was Olivia’s triumphant smile as the world faded to black.

**~~**

** **
He awoke to agony. It was not the pain of a wound, but a holistic, searing torment, as if every fiber of his being was being unmade by an unseen fire. Sunlight. A single, merciless beam lanced through a crack in the stone ceiling of the crypt he found himself in. He recoiled with a hiss he didn't recognize, scrambling into the deepest shadows. The charm, the blissful adoration for Olivia, was shattered, leaving behind the bitter, filthy residue of its violation.

His analytical mind, his greatest tool and defense, took over. *Assess the situation. Location: Unknown crypt, likely within the Suzail cemetery. Status: Alive. No, not alive. Altered.* He ran a hand over his face. His bronze skin, once kissed by the Cormanthor sun, was now the pale, anemic color of old parchment. He caught his reflection in a shard of polished marble lid. His hair, a river of gold, had bleached to a stark, platinum blonde. And his eyes… his familiar silver eyes now held flickering, malevolent embers of red.

Fangs, long and sharp, rested against his lower lip. He was a vampire. An abomination.

A surge of revulsion and despair threatened to drown him. He, a scion of House Durothil, a sun elf, a child of Corellon's light, was now a creature of shadow and foul thirst. As if to mock him, a single, stubborn root had pushed its way through the stone floor. Instinctively, he reached out, his elven soul yearning for the familiar comfort of a living thing.

The moment his fingers brushed the root, it withered. The green vitality vanished, leaving a dry, blackened husk that crumbled to dust. A cry of pure anguish tore from his throat. The Black Thumb. A curse he’d only read about in forbidden texts. His intimate connection to the living world, the very essence of being Tel'Quessir, was severed. He was poison to the earth he once cherished.

** **
The psychological agony of his sundered connection to nature was a blow more profound than any physical wound. He, Selanar Durothil, a sun elf of Myth Drannor, was now a blight upon the world. The pain was so sharp it almost overshadowed the other, subtler changes. Almost. As the day wore on in the suffocating stillness of the crypt, his analytical mind began to claw its way back from the abyss of despair. This was a condition. A curse. And like any magical phenomenon, it must have rules, parameters, and weaknesses. He was a Seeker of Mysteries, and he had just become the most terrible mystery of all.

He spent the daylight hours in a state of grim self-examination. His senses were unnaturally sharp; he could hear the scuttling of a spider in the far corner of the crypt, smell the cloying decay of ancient funeral silks. He felt a new strength humming in his limbs, a cold, predatory power that was utterly alien to the subtle grace of elven magic. When dusk finally painted the sky in shades of bruised purple and blood orange, a new instinct stirred within him. A desire to move, to hunt, to escape the confines of his tomb. He thought of the night sky, of the freedom of flight, and a strange, pulling sensation twisted his body. Bones reshaped, flesh flowed like wax, and with a final, disorienting wrench, he was no longer an elf. He was a bat, hanging upside-down from the sarcophagus lid, his world a dizzying sonar map of clicks and echoes.

*Fascinating*, the analytical part of his mind noted, even as the rest of him recoiled.

**~~**

** **
He knew he couldn't stay in Suzail. Olivia had abandoned him here, a cruel experiment left to its own devices. Arabel was his only hope. Amythyst, with her casual mastery of forces beyond mortal ken, was the only person he could imagine confronting with this horror. But the journey was long, and the sun was his new, implacable enemy. He recalled vampire lore from his studies: they needed the soil of their grave to rest. With a shudder of revulsion, he transformed back, his platinum hair falling around his pale face. Using a loose stone, he pried open the sarcophagus he had awoken in and scooped the dry, musty grave dirt into the Haversack Amythyst had given him. He was literally carrying his own grave.

The first night of travel was a trial of will. Every rustle in the trees, every scent on the wind, screamed of life, and a gnawing emptiness began to ache in his gut. The thirst. It was a faint echo at first, a memory of Olivia's bite, but it grew steadily with each passing hour. He flew through the night, a dark speck against the moon, landing before dawn to find a secluded, high branch. There, he'd transform into a cold mist, seeping into the Haversack to burrow into the grave dirt, the touch of it a grotesque comfort against the coming day.

On the second night, the thirst was no longer an ache; it was a physical craving, a fire in his veins that eclipsed all thought. The line from his personal creed twisted into a monstrous new meaning: *“Lust is the craving for salt of a man who is dying of thirst.”* He was dying of thirst, and the world was a feast.

** **
That was when he saw him. A lone merchant, slumped in his saddle, his horse plodding wearily along the road. Selanar's analytical mind screamed for restraint. *Observe. Control. Take only what you need. A deer, a wolf, not a person.* But the Beast, this new, hungry thing coiled in his soul, roared back. The scent of warm blood, the rhythmic pulse of a living heart—it was an irresistible symphony.

He didn't remember deciding to attack. One moment he was a bat clinging to a branch, the next he was behind the man, his new form moving with silent, impossible speed. His fangs sank into the man’s neck, and the world exploded in sensation. It wasn't just blood; it was life, warmth, memories, emotions, all flooding into him in a blissful, intoxicating torrent. The taste! Gods, the taste! It was the finest wine, the most decadent feast, the most profound pleasure he had ever known. The analytical mind was not just stuffed into a closet; it was bound, gagged, and thrown into a dungeon while the Beast sated a hunger it hadn't known it possessed.

When he finally pulled away, the man was a lifeless sack on the dusty road. The hunger was gone, replaced by a cold, leaden horror. He looked at his hands, at the being he had become. A killer. A predator. He had studied demons, taking notes on their anatomy with detached curiosity. Now, he understood their nature intimately. He was one of them.

The Beast was sated, but the Elf was screaming. Selanar stood over the cooling body, the coppery tang of life still on his tongue, and a wave of self-loathing so profound it nearly brought him to his knees washed over him. He was a defiler, a murderer. The analytical mind, once a tool for uncovering the universe's secrets, now turned inward with cold, clinical horror. Observation: The subject has lost control. Primary drive: bloodlust. Result: termination of a sentient life. Conclusion: The current methodology of survival is unsustainable and morally bankrupt.

**~~**

** **
Arabel. He had to get to Arabel. But the thought of another slow, torturous journey, another battle against the rising thirst, was unbearable. There had to be another way. And then, the knowledge surfaced, a dark pearl dredged from the depths of his arcane studies. Elf vampires… can travel through plants… 

The irony was a physical pain, a cruel joke played by a merciless cosmos. To escape his monstrous nature, he would have to use one of its most monstrous abilities. A way to travel, to move through the very heart of the world he could no longer touch without killing. A power that left a trail of dead, withered giants in its wake. He, who had felt the life force of the great forests as an extension of his own soul, would now become a plague upon them.

But desperation was a stronger acid than irony. Cormanthyr. Before Arabel, he had to try. His home. Surely the accumulated wisdom of the Tel'Quessir, the power of the High Magi of his own house, could offer some hope, some sliver of a chance at redemption. It was a fool's hope, born of his ingrained belief in the glory of his people, but it was the only hope he had.

Choosing a massive, ancient oak, a patriarch of the forest that had likely witnessed the city’s first founding, he felt a wave of profound sorrow. In his former life, he would have admired its stoicism, felt the slow, steady pulse of its life. "Forgive me," he whispered to the night air, his breath a cold mist. Pulling on a pair of fine leather gloves—a new, necessary affectation—he laid a hand on its rough bark. It was a farewell to a stranger he was about to murder.

Closing his eyes, he focused on the innate power now coiled within him. He envisioned the living network of roots spreading through Faerûn, a secret web he could now perceive. He thought of Cormanthor, of the great oaks that stood sentinel at the edge of the Elven Court. He stepped forward.

** **
The world did not dissolve into a portal's shimmering light. It was a dissolution, a surrendering of form. The world of solid matter melted away into a vertiginous, green-and-brown tunnel of pure life energy. For a fleeting, agonizing moment, he felt the oak’s life force—the slow, patient drawing of water, the silent communion with the sun, the centuries of memory stored in its rings. It was a connection he craved more than blood, and his very presence was extinguishing it like a cold flame. He felt the tree’s life shudder and die around him as he passed through it.

He emerged from a sister oak within sight of the spires of Myth Drannor, stumbling out onto the forest floor. He turned back, his silver-red eyes wide with horror. The second oak, vibrant and full of life just moments before, now looked… weary. A faint greyness was creeping into its leaves. Within a week, it too would be dead. He had used his home as a tool and killed it in the process.

He stumbled out into moonlight, the air different, purer. He was in Cormanthor. The air itself, thick with the magic of the ancient mythal, felt like breathing glass shards. The very essence of his homeland rejected him. He took another step, and another, leaving a trail of dead oaks across the continent, a path of arboreal corpses marking his desperate pilgrimage. Each transit was another stab of guilt, another confirmation of his corruption.

He kept to the shadows of his own forest, his hood pulled low to hide his ghastly pallor and platinum hair. He made his way to the Durothil estate, its familiar, gracefully grown spires a painful mockery of the beauty he could no longer truly feel. He did not dare approach the main entrance. Instead, he found a secluded grove and sent a silent, magical message, a simple summons woven with a thread of their shared bloodline, meant only for his brother.

** **
Taenis arrived, his warrior's posture alert, hand resting on the pommel of his sword. *"Selanar? What is this? Why the secrecy?"*

Selanar stepped from the shadows. For a moment, Taenis just stared. The silence was absolute, broken only by the whisper of the wind through leaves that Selanar could no longer feel. Taenis, ever the pragmatist, the soldier, took in the changes with a clinical eye: the dead-white skin, the faint, predatory glow in the silver eyes, the subtle but undeniable scent of the tomb that clung to him.

*"By Corellon's tears… what have they done to you?"* Taenis whispered, his voice laced with horror and grief.

*"I need help, brother,"* Selanar's voice was a dry rasp. *"The High Magi… there must be a ritual, a cure…"*

Taenis took a half-step back, his expression hardening from sorrow to grim duty. *"There is no cure for this, Selanar. You are… anathema. You blight the very ground you stand on. The Mythal itself cries out at your presence."* He looked at his brother, tears in his eyes. *"You have to leave."*

** **
Under the cloak of deepest night, he made his way to a place he knew would be secluded: a lesser shrine to Corellon, tucked away in a quiet glade. He did not dare approach the main temples. He found a priest, an old, wizened elf he recognized from his youth, tending to the moon-silvered flowers.

When the priest saw him step from the shadows, he gasped, his hand instinctively going to the holy symbol around his neck. Selanar's appearance was a blasphemy in this sacred place—the pale skin, the predatory glow in his eyes, the subtle aura of undeath that clung to him.

*"Faern… Selanar?"* the priest whispered, his voice trembling with disbelief and fear.

*"Help me,"* Selanar rasped, the words tearing at his throat. He dropped to his knees, his pride stripped away, leaving only desperation. He explained everything, the capture, the turning, the thirst, the terrible curse of his touch. He held out his gloved hands as if they were covered in poison. *"I am… a blight. An abomination. Is there any cure? Any ritual? Can the Seldarine wash this stain from my soul?"*

The old priest listened, his initial fear replaced by a deep, profound pity. He examined Selanar, not with magic, but with the sad, ancient wisdom of his people. After a long silence that stretched for an eternity, he shook his head slowly, his eyes filled with sorrow.

*"The blessing of Corellon is life, my child. What has been done to you is a twisting of that life, a knot woven into your very soul that we have no power to untangle,"* the priest said softly. *"High Magic itself might struggle with such a curse. Your life, as you knew it, is over. We have no cure for you here."*

The words were a final, devastating blow. Hope, a flickering ember he hadn't even realized he was still clutching, was extinguished. His own people, his own faith, had no answer. He was truly, irrevocably damned.

** **
*"But,"* the priest continued, seeing the utter despair on Selanar's face, *"damnation is not the only path. You still possess your mind, your will. The path of atonement is long and arduous, but it is never closed. I can place a **geas** upon you, if you wish. A binding oath to hold the Beast at bay, to guide you towards a purpose greater than your hunger. To seek a true cure, however faint the hope, and to harm no innocent soul along the way."*

A ***geas***. A magical command to reinforce his crumbling will. It was a crutch, not a cure, but it was something. It was a leash for the monster he had become.

*"Do it,"* Selanar said, his voice hollow. *"Bind me. Before I become something that can no longer ask for it."*

He bowed his head, accepting the priest's ritualistic touch. The magic settled over him, a cold, heavy weight of obligation. He was an exile, not just from the sun, but from his own kind. His quest for the Quess’Ar’Teranthvar and the Ary’Faern’Kerym seemed a bitter, distant memory, the ambitions of a dead man. Now, his only quest was to survive the night, and perhaps, one day, find a way to truly die. With the priest's grim blessing and the weight of the *geas* on his soul, he turned his back on his home and made his way towards Arabel. Amythyst was his last, desperate hope.

**~~**

** **
The journey back from Cormanthor was a slow, deliberate torture. The ***geas*** was not a comfort, but a cold iron collar around his soul, a constant, chilling reminder of the Beast that strained against it. He still used that terrible, life-drinking power, to cross vast distances, but each transit was a fresh wound on his conscience, a new ghost-tree added to the silent, withered forest that now marked his path across Faerûn. The pain of it was a fitting penance.

During the nights he spent on foot, the world was a different place. His elven eyes, once attuned to the subtle beauty of starlight on leaves, were now instruments of predation. The world was a symphony of heartbeats. The frantic drumming of a rabbit hiding in a briar patch, the slow, steady rhythm of a sleeping doe, the distant thrum of a farmer in his bed—each was a distinct note that called to the emptiness inside him.

The thirst returned, as he knew it would. One night, he came upon a small camp, a family of woodcutters huddled around a dying fire. The scent of their sleeping forms, of the warm blood moving just beneath their skin, was overwhelming. The Beast roared. His fangs ached, his muscles tensed to spring, and the analytical mind offered a dozen ways to take them all without a sound.

Then the ***geas*** struck. It was not a gentle reminder, but a spike of pure psychic agony, a mental shriek that drowned out the Beast's roar. The pain was so intense he collapsed to his knees, clutching his head. ***Harm no innocent soul.*** The command burned through his consciousness, a brand of holy fire on his damned spirit. Gasping, sweating a cold, dead sweat, he forced himself to his feet and fled into the night, the agony of the ***geas*** a more immediate terror than the hunger itself.

** **
His analytical mind, now bent towards the grim science of his own survival, began to formulate a plan. First, sustenance. He couldn't rely on the thin blood of beasts forever; it was a losing battle. He recalled ancient lore, tales of fey creatures and their life-giving magic. ***"Goodberries."*** Could a creature of undeath consume something of pure, natural life? He found a druid's grove, a place he would have once revered, and, keeping to the deepest shadows, he watched a young acolyte perform the simple ritual. After the druid left, Selanar slipped in, his gloved hand hesitating over the small cluster of crimson berries.

Plucking one, he brought it to his lips. The moment it touched his tongue, it was not the sweet burst of flavor he remembered, but a searing fire. It was like swallowing a drop of molten sunlight. The pain was excruciating, a violent rejection of life by his dead flesh, but through the agony, he felt something else: a flicker of satiation. The gnawing emptiness receded, not fully, but enough. It was a new form of self-flagellation, a painful but necessary penance to keep the Beast in chains.

Second, control. If the ***geas*** was the chain, his own will had to be the lock. He considered the flaw from his notes: the insatiable desire for carnal pleasures. It had led him to this. Now, he would turn it into a weapon against himself. If the thirst became truly unbearable, if the agony of the goodberries was not enough, he had one last resort. He would bite his own wrist, drink his own dead, stagnant blood. The thought was vile, but it was a lesser evil. He would feed the monster on itself before he allowed it to prey on the world.

**~~**

** **
Finally, he reached the outskirts of Arabel. The bustling, living city was now a fortress he had to infiltrate. He could not walk through the gates; his pallor and glowing eyes would bring the guard down on him in moments. Waiting for the deepest point of the night, he found a quiet alley, let his form dissolve into that of a bat, and took to the sky.

He flew over the walls he had once defended, a silent, leathery shadow against the moon. He made a beeline for the one place that represented his only remaining hope: Carlson's Apothecary. The shop was dark, save for the faint, magical glow of potions on the shelves. Slipping down the chimney, a classic and undignified maneuver he never thought he’d perform, he landed softly on the hearth in a cloud of soot.

He resumed his elven form, a pale ghost in the familiar, chaotic shop. He saw the great pink bubble she favored floating near the ceiling. He saw the powerful reagents and the half-finished experiments. It was a place of potent, wild magic, and he, a creature now anathema to life, felt utterly out of place. 

As Selanar’s eyes adjusted to the dim, potion-lit interior of the apothecary, a movement near the counter startled him. A hulking brown bear, Barnaby, lifted his massive head, his nostrils twitching. The bear’s low growl was not aggressive, but confused, a deep rumble that vibrated through the floorboards. He smelled elf, he smelled Durothil, but it was tainted with the cloying sweetness of the grave and the cold iron of spilled blood.

Selanar froze, his hand instinctively moving towards a spell that was no longer there, replaced by a cold, predatory urge. He forced it down. This was Amythyst’s guardian, and he was a supplicant here, not a threat. He raised his gloved hands in a gesture of peace. The bear watched him, its intelligent eyes filled with suspicion, but it did not advance.

** **
A shimmer of blue light suddenly illuminated the back room, casting long, dancing shadows across the shop. The air crackled with the ozone scent of teleportation magic, and a circular portal tore open in reality. Out stepped Amythyst.

She didn't look surprised to see him, which was perhaps the most surprising thing of all. Her emerald eyes, sharp and discerning, swept over him in a single, comprehensive glance. She took in the deathly pale skin, the platinum-blonde hair shimmering in the magical light, and the crimson embers that now warred with the silver in his eyes. She saw the new, rigid posture, the way he held himself as if constantly bracing against an internal assault. She saw the ghost of the man he was, and the monster he had become.

*"Olivia,"* she said, her voice flat. It wasn't a question.

Selanar could only nod, his throat tight. Words failed him. The Seeker of Mysteries, the bon viveur with a silver tongue, was mute before her calm, knowing gaze.

*"The hunger,"* she stated, her eyes flicking to the pulse beating in her own throat, then back to his face. *"Nearly all-consuming, isn't it?"*

He swallowed, the simple mechanical act feeling alien. *"I… I am managing,"* he rasped, the words sounding weak and pathetic even to his own ears. He felt the Beast stir at her proximity, at the vibrant life force she radiated, and he fought it down with a surge of will fortified by the cold fire of the ***geas***.

** **
*"I can see that,"* she replied, her tone softening with something that might have been pity. She moved behind her counter, her movements fluid and unhurried, as if entertaining a vampire in her shop at midnight was a common occurrence. *"You should understand,"* she began, her voice gentle but firm, *"I have nothing that can help you. Not truly. This isn't a curse I can unravel with a spell or brew into a potion. Brianna… Wolfane’s daughter… Olivia’s spawn got to her. She arrived here, bound in ropes, and I… I had to stake her. She’s with the priestess at the Temple of Ilmater now, trapped in a state between life and death. Even she, with all her divine grace, doesn't know the spells required to save her. Or you."*

Each word was a hammer blow to Selanar's last, fragile hopes. He had known, logically, that a simple cure was unlikely, but to have it stated so plainly, by her, was a final death sentence.

Amythyst leaned forward, her expression turning serious. *"All I can offer you is this cold comfort,"* she said, her voice low. *"Revenge. A purpose. Your friends are not here. They are at Montague's fortress, fighting for their lives against Olivia's forces. But the true architect of Highmoon's fall, the one Olivia serves in that theater of the war, is a Red Wizard of Thay. A particularly nasty bitch. She and her forces are gathering to the northeast, preparing their next strike."*

She slid a small vial across the counter. It was thin, crystalline, and filled with a swirling, silvery-orange liquid that seemed to capture and hold the light.

** **
*"Go to them. Take wing, fly to the northeast. Find this Red Wizard. Kill her. Kill them all. You have the power for it now. Find some measure of peace in vengeance, before all you know is the hunger."* Her eyes met his, and he saw a flicker of genuine sorrow in their depths. *"Afterward, if you wish for an end, drink this."* She tapped the vial. *"It will grant you one final sunrise. A true death. Unless,"* she added softly, *"you want to live, or unlive, the rest of your existence in eternal darkness."*

The choice was laid bare before him. A final, bloody purpose, followed by oblivion. Or an eternity as this… thing. He looked at the vial, the promise of release shimmering within. He looked at his gloved hands, remembering the feel of withering life beneath his touch.

*"My friends… at the fortress?"* he asked, his voice stronger now, latching onto the one solid thing in his shattered world.

*"Yes,"* Amythyst confirmed. *"Fighting Olivia's pets as we speak. You could go to them. Help them. But know this, Selanar."* Her gaze was piercing. *"Every day you exist on this… diet… a part of you dies. Your connection to who you were frays. The hunger will grow, and one day, the *geas* and your will won't be enough. One day, you will look at your friends and see nothing but a meal."*

A flicker of the old Selanar, the obstinate seeker who believed in infinite possibility, pushed back against the crushing weight of Amythyst's pragmatism. Oblivion was an answer, but it was an end to the mystery, not a solution. Vengeance was a purpose, but a hollow one, a final spasm of rage before the fade. No. His entire life had been a refutation of finality.

** **
*There is always a way. Somewhere. Somehow.*

*"No,"* he said, the word tasting strange and powerful on his tongue. He pushed the vial of silvery-orange liquid gently back across the counter towards her. *"An end is a concession. I will not concede."*

Amythyst raised an eyebrow, a spark of intrigue in her emerald eyes. *"You would choose this eternity? A constant war against yourself?"*

*"I would choose to **search**,"* Selanar corrected, his voice gaining a measure of its former conviction. *"You say there is no cure you know of. But the cosmos is vast, Amythyst. The Quess'Ar'Teranthvar spoke of the creation of new life. The ancient magic of Arselu’Tel’Quess… it wove the very fabric of reality. Somewhere in that lost knowledge, somewhere in the forgotten corners of the planes, there must be a way to unweave this curse. Or, if not, to rewrite it."*

He was no longer just a Seeker of Mysteries; he was the subject of the greatest mystery he had ever encountered. His quest had not ended; it had been horrifically personalized. To restore the ancient arts was no longer an academic pursuit for the glory of his people. It was now the only path to restoring his own soul.

*"I will not be a monster,"* he declared, more to himself than to her. *"And I will not die as one. I will find an answer."*

A slow, wry smile touched Amythyst’s lips. It was a smile of respect for a kind of defiant foolishness she understood all too well. *"The Elven Empress didn't save you. The priests of Corellon couldn't. I can't. You place your faith in a ghost of a chance, Selanar."*

** **
*"Faith is all I have left,"* he admitted. *"That, and a very specific set of new and unpleasant skills."* His gaze sharpened. *"My friends are at Montague's Fortress. Olivia is there. She took something from me. I will help them take something from her."*

He was already formulating a plan. He was a creature of the night, a master of stealth and fear. He could be the unseen blade, the terrifying whisper in the dark that turned the tide. He would use the monster's tools to protect the living, and in doing so, prove to himself—and to the universe—that he was still Selanar Durothil.

*"Good,"* Amythyst said, nodding once. She retrieved the vial and tucked it away. *"But be warned,"* she added, not looking up from her work. *"Your clock is ticking. You are strong-willed, Selanar. Your intellect is a powerful shield. But every time you give in to the thirst, every life you take, the shield cracks. Fail too many times, and the man you are now will be gone, replaced only by the hunger. Olivia will have won."*

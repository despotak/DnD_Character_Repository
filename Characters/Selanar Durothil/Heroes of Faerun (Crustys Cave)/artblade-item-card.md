# Ary'Faern'Kerym — the Artblade
### *"The Noble Sorcerer's Sword"* — 5e translation (Vestige of Divergence)

> **Design contract.** Sources: *Cormanthyr: Empire of Elves* (2e, pp. 154–156 — the Elfblades chapter and blade-rite rituals) and *Lost Empires of Faerûn* (3e, pp. 157–159 — the elfblades as major artifacts). Every power below is a prior-edition ability mapped through a 5e analogue; the balance annex shows the mapping. The **Vestige of Divergence** framework (*Explorer's Guide to Wildemount*) is used not as a convenience but because it *is* the canon: an elfblade whose wielder dies **goes inert and nonmagical until drawn again in a blade-rite or at a place of power** (2e, verbatim mechanic). Dormant → Awakened → Exalted simply gives that resurrection a 5e grammar. All state transitions are **DM-triggered story beats.**

## The lore, in full

### The Forging (c. −4000 DR)

Before Cormanthyr, there were four elven realms scattered through the great forest of Arcorar — Elven Court, Semberholme, Uvaeren, Jhyrennstar — and they were dying separately: a meteor took Uvaeren's lore-houses, the drow bled Elven Court from below. The Coronal **Oacenth of Jhyrennstar**, a prophet of Labelas Enoreth, lay dying of a wasting illness, and his last great act was a commission: **three elfblades**, forged and enspelled by High Magic, to choose the rulers who would unite the tribes.

- **Ar'Cor'Kerym, the Crownblade** — the Rulers' Blade, to name the **Coronal**. Kahvoerm Irithyl drew it and became the first ruler of unified Cormanthyr.
- **Ary'Velahr'Kerym, the Warblade** — to name the **Arms-Major**, commander of the Akh'Velahr, the army.
- **Ary'Faern'Kerym, the Artblade** — to name the **Spell-Major**, the *Defender by Art*: commander of the **Akh'Faer**, the arcane arm of the military, and keeper of the realm's magical defenses.

The blades choose. No politics, no inheritance, no assassination has ever shortcut a blade-rite — would-be usurpers learned, to their lasting shame, that ruthlessness itself is disqualifying.

### The blade itself

A longsword — though the smiths of Jhyrennstar never asked human categories for permission; the surviving sketches show a **single-edged, lightly curved blade with a guarded hilt**. It appears **forged from solid darkness**: a void of nonreflective blackness that feels solid as stone yet slick as oil. Along the razor edge runs a **thin crimson line of energy** enclosing the dark — the only light the blade owns. When drawn, it **crackles like lightning breaking across a metal shield**; when swung, it emits a sharp **buzzing like an angry bee** (the wielder can mute it at will); when it strikes, the clash is **utterly silent**, no matter the force behind the blow. Embossed in glowing crimson at the base is the elven rune used to signify a work of High Magic.

### The record of its bearers — and the two wounds

**450 DR — the Durothil wound.** The Spell-Major **Zaos Durothil**, youngest wielder in the blade's history at **122 winters**, was carried off — sorely wounded, sword in hand — by the red dragon **Edallisufanxar, "the Burning Blaze,"** during an assault on the wyrm's Cormanthyran lair. The dragon had spent **250 years** scheming to take the sword from the boy's father before it took the boy instead. They were last seen winging south and west toward the **Giant's Run Mountains**. Neither fate was ever recorded. For ten centuries the Art Tower flew its banners over a borrowed sword: **Faervian**, a baneblade forged by the human wizard Demron — an admission, in steel, that the true blade was gone.

**500–674 DR — the sword that chooses.** The noble bladesinger **Josidiah Starym** left Cormanthor on a legend's errand: to recover the *Warblade* from beneath the drow-held Twisted Tower. He returned 174 years later carrying **the Artblade instead** — where and how, the histories never say. The lesson the People took: *an elfblade is not found; it consents to be found.* Josidiah bore it as Spell-Major, and the rank-tattoo of that office — azure lines, four circles, lozenges, silver on green — was last worn on his forearm.

**714 DR — the second loss.** At the **Battle of Stars Shining**, with the Army of Darkness's yugoloths storming Myth Drannor's school of magic and his forces breaking, Spell-Major Josidiah Starym cast a spell of terrible power. **Pure white flame** consumed most of the yugoloth host and much of the school. When it died, **no trace of the Spell-Major or the sword remained.** Sages have theorized Dweomerheart, or Arvandor. No evidence supports either.

### Its sisters

The **Crownblade** was last seen in 666 DR, vanishing with the Srinshee after she rebuilt the Rule Tower — scholars believe it removed itself, and her, from Faerûn ahead of the city's fall. *(Those who stood at Myth Drannor's second fall in 1487 and saw the Srinshee come and go may know more than the scholars do.)* The **Warblade** lies where it fell in −331 DR, beneath the **Twisted Tower**, paralyzed by drow magic beside the body of Lord Orym Hawksong; more than a hundred elves have gone down after it. None returned.

### The blade today (1495 DR)

By the law of its own making, the Artblade is **inert** — a dead length of dark metal wherever it lies, invisible to scrying that hunts for magic, waiting to be *drawn*: in a blade-rite governed by High Magic, or at a place of power. Restoring even one elfblade to the People would earn "the everlasting friendship and thanks of the Elven Court" (*Lost Empires of Faerûn*) — and the rebuilt realm's Coronal, in exile at Semberholme, has rather fewer relics of legitimacy than her predecessors did.

Three threads point toward it. A red dragon's hoard somewhere under the Giant's Run Mountains, never plundered. A white flame that consumed a school of magic without leaving ash. And a certain High Lore Gem which — in its final, blistering-red state — *knows where the Artblade sleeps. It will not say. It will show him, when the People need it.*

## The item

*Weapon (longsword), artifact (Vestige of Divergence) — requires attunement by an elf or half-elf spellcaster of good alignment, by blade-rite (below)*

**Sentience.** The Artblade is a sentient weapon (**INT 16, WIS 14, CHA 18**; hearing and darkvision 120 ft). It speaks Elvish aloud in a low, buzzing thrum and communicates telepathically with its wielder. It is **conscious only while drawn**. Alignment: **neutral good**. Purpose: *the realm of Cormanthyr first; the wielder second.* It desires a bearer with a passion for the Art and a **selfless love of Cormanthyr and the People** — and it has refused kings. Should a wielder stray from the blade's purpose or alignment, it may re-impose the blade-rite on the spot, inflict stinging refusals (1d4 psychic damage on a held blade), or contest control (2024 sentient-item conflict rules; treat its Charisma as unusually forceful — canon rates its will at the maximum the old rules could record).

**The Blade-Rite (attunement).** Attunement is not chosen; it is *survived*. Grasping the hilt with intent to bond triggers three trials **of talent, mind, and heart**, experienced within the aspirant's mind over the course of a short rest, real in every way that matters — the DM stages them (suggested mechanical spine: an Intelligence saving throw, a Wisdom saving throw, and an Arcana or spellcasting-themed test, DCs set by the story). **Failure divorces the aspirant from the Weave**: they can cast no spells until the next dawn and suffer one level of exhaustion, and the blade will not consider them again until they have *changed* — in ages past the severance lasted years, and the blade remembers how to hold a grudge. A **non-elf, or a bearer of evil intent**, who grasps the drawn blade takes **3d12 psychic damage** (repeats each round it is held) and cannot attune. *(Softened from the 2e permanent-damage/death and the 3e 1d6-year severance; teeth kept.)*

### ◆ Dormant — "the Mourning Blade" *(from the successful blade-rite)*

*Newly drawn after eight centuries, the blade is grief-quiet: the crimson edge burns low, the voice comes seldom.*

- **+1 longsword.** Its strikes are silent; its buzz is the wielder's to mute or loose.
- **Blade of the Faer:** the sword is a **spellcasting focus** for the wielder's arcane spells.
- **Lightless and Light:** the wielder controls the blade's glow at will — from utterly dark to **bright light in a 30-foot radius** (the "daylight of highsun," 2e).
- **Sense the Art:** the wielder can cast ***detect magic*** at will (no slot; the blade hums toward dweomers).
- **The Bond:** as a bonus action, the wielder can call the blade to hand from up to **100 feet** away.
- **Battle-Tempo:** **+2 bonus to initiative rolls** *(2e, verbatim — flagged in the annex: this table rerolls initiative every round, which makes the rider stronger here than at most tables).*

### ◆ Awakened — "the Defender by Art" *(DM story beat; suggested: the wielder can cast 5th-level spells and has bled for the People)*

*The crimson edge brightens; the blade begins to speak of the Akh'Faer as "we."*

All previous, and:

- **+2 longsword.**
- **Casting Through the Blade:** while wielding the Artblade, the wielder's spells channel *through* it — **somatic components become sword-forms and verbal components become the blade's keening** (casting is indistinguishable from swordplay to observers, requires no free hand, and non-costly material components are not needed; *silence* still stops the keening). *(2e/3e core power: Still + Silent + Eschew Materials. At a table where "verbal is loud and somatic is flashy," this is the Spell-Major's signature — the Art hidden inside the dance.)*
- **Unweaving:** cast ***dispel magic*** once per day without a slot (at 3rd level).
- **The Bond, Perfected:** the call-to-hand works across **any distance on the same plane**, through walls and wards *(canon; specific barriers at DM's discretion)*.

### ◆ Exalted — "the Spell-Major" *(DM story beat; suggested: formally named Spell-Major — the rank is the power)*

*The edge burns like a wound in the world. The rank-tattoo of the office turns silver on green.*

All previous, and:

- **+3 longsword.**
- **The Art, Magnified:** the wielder's **evocation spells** are cast as if using a spell slot **one level higher** (no additional cost; slots of 9th level are not exceeded). *(2e/3e "+1 caster level for evocations," translated.)*
- **Unweaving, Perfected:** ***dispel magic*** three times per day, cast at **5th level**.
- **The Long Noon:** the wielder does not age while bonded, and up to a century of past age falls away over the following year *(2e's 1d6×100-year restoration, tempered)*.
- **The Rank:** the wielder *is* the Spell-Major of Cormanthyr — command of the Akh'Faer's survivors and remnants, custody of the realm's magical defenses, a voice beside the Coronal. This is the blade's greatest power and it has no dice attached; it never did.

## Balance annex

| Power | Prior-edition source | 5e analogue / rarity |
|---|---|---|
| +1/+2/+3 by state | 2e "+4 attack/damage" flattened | Standard vestige weapon ramp |
| Focus + detect magic at will | 2e at-will power | Common–uncommon rider |
| Light control 0–30 ft | 2e, verbatim | Flavor + utility |
| +2 initiative | 2e, verbatim | Weapon of Warning analogue (uncommon) — **inflated by per-round initiative rerolls at this table; strike it if that reads too strong** |
| Call to hand (100 ft → plane-wide) | 2e bond, verbatim | Returning-weapon-plus; story-proof against disarm/theft |
| Casting through the blade (S/V/M) | 2e/3e core power, verbatim | Subtle-Spell-as-identity; the single strongest rider here, gated to Awakened |
| Dispel 1/day → 3/day @5th | 2e/3e "dispel 3/day" | Rationed at Dormant/Awakened, canon-full at Exalted |
| Evocation +1 slot level | 2e/3e "+1 CL evocations" | Exalted-only, no-cost upcast — legendary-tier rider |
| No aging / restoration | 2e/3e, tempered | Flavor (elves hardly need it) |
| 2e's +2 AC | — | **Deliberately dropped** — Bladesong owns the AC lane; double-dipping would stack the wrong way |
| Spell-Major rank | All editions | Narrative. Priceless, diceless. |

**Why a DM can say yes:** it enters play *inert* and worthless without a story-gated rite; the DM controls every state transition; its best mechanical rider (component-free casting) arrives mid-tier on an artifact the campaign's own endgame is built around; and the blade itself is an NPC with veto power — a wielder the sword stops trusting is a plot, not a stat block. Total combat footprint at Exalted ≈ a +3 weapon with utility riders; the artifact label prices the *office*, not the numbers.

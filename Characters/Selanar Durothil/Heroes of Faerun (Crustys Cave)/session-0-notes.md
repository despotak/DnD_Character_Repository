## 4. Content Boundaries
- **No sexual content whatsoever** — uncomfortable for everyone
- **No PvP without pre-consulted consent**
  - Stealing/fighting/tricking other players
  - Anything involving a contested roll against each other is PvP
  - Bad for morale, no one likes that
- **Don't be a murder hobo**
  - Killing whoever you want will ruin the campaign
  - Realistic consequences
- **Cooperation over isolation**
  - Splitting the party is fine narratively, but having a place to meet and others knowing where you are/what you're doing is essential for regrouping
  - Think about how the party will find you when shit hits the fan
- **Content expectations:** gritty, real-world issues — corruption, slavery, classism

## 5. Expectations for the Campaign
- Mystery/intrigue heavy — keep that in mind with the skills you choose; lesser-chosen skills will be useful (help action only when you have proficiency)
- Combats are difficult — people go down, your character may die. Generally higher damage across the board
- Pace — expect to level up every 3–5 sessions, milestone leveling
- Game time — usually runs 3.5–4 hours
- Give a heads-up if you're going to miss a session
  - Narrative reasoning for your absence
  - Party has access to your skills/abilities but no one will play your character — they're "off camera," won't be in combat, things get rescaled
- Missing sessions — with 3 players the game still goes on; regular absences risk removal from the game

## 6. House Rules
- Monster stat blocks are modified and heavily changed (e.g. a ghost won't take damage when in your space, you will; changed typing/resistance/vulnerability, etc.)
- Encumbrance isn't tightly tracked — general realism, no carrying a hundred swords
- Shopping happens off camera unless there's narrative purpose
- Ammunition isn't tracked — bogs down the game
- Components — gold cost for material components must be purchased; verbal components are loud (can't be whispered), somatic components are flashy (can't be done behind your back)
- Survival rules aren't micromanaged — assumed skilled, well-traveled adventurers carry what they need
- Rests
  - Long rests require a safe, secure location — hard to recover deep in a dungeon
  - Short rests as normal; downtime used if space/availability allows
- **Dirty Critical** — 10+ over the AC counts as a critical. Speeds up combat, enemies hit harder, so do you. Does not affect "on critical hit" abilities. Because of this, adamantine armour/weapons are not allowed
- **Dynamic initiative** — rerolled every combat round (automatic in Foundry), changes how start/end-of-turn abilities work. Only feat changed: Alert can be used Proficiency Bonus times
- **Health potions** — restore to full health, used as an action
- **Boons** — everyone gets a boon at 19th level regardless of multiclassing; player-specific boons handed out at higher levels
- **Magic items** — attunement slots = Proficiency Bonus; most magic items given out are player-specific

## 7. Campaign Setting
- Canonical Forgotten Realms — 1495 D.R.
- Everything is canon; may adjust to fit the campaign if needed
- Notable named heroes (Elminster, Drizzt, etc.) are not acknowledged in play — "Superman stays out of Gotham"

## 8. Character Creation Notes
- Investigation plays a big part — all skills matter, don't build a character that's only good at combat or you'll take a backseat during narrative play
- Don't over-optimize the build — a cool character is more fun than an effective one; combats are balanced accordingly
- When swapping tool proficiencies, pick something you'll actually use during downtime
- **Rolling for stats:** 4d6kh3, rolled 5 times; the 6th stat is the total sum vs. 75. Roll 3 arrays total — you'll have access to everyone's unchosen arrays. Leads to slightly higher-than-average stats with more build diversity, esp. for MAD classes
- Because stats run higher, lean toward feats over ASIs — ASIs are boring
- Very flexible on backgrounds/feat-linked ASIs — swapping to a different feat or stat is fine

## 9. Level Zero
- Campaign spans from the very start of your journey to becoming a Hero — first session or two cover how/why your character has the abilities and powers they do
- Every class ability/feat should have a reasonable in-fiction explanation — keep that in mind while building your character
- Hitting level 3 / subclass narratively has a big story impact — have a plan for your build's direction
- The first time an ability/power is revealed, there's in-fiction context for why (e.g. a druidic awakening — hearing plants/trees warn of an incoming threat; witnessing someone important get stabbed and praying for the first time as Cure Wounds triggers)
- Characters are built as 1st-level characters, but abilities get dropped in narratively as they fit the story (everyone still levels up together mechanically)
- A compelling character arc involves transformation — the more detail on home, origins, and important people in your character's life, the more the GM has to weave into the shared narrative

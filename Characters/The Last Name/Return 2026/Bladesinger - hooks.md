# Bladesinger: hooks for Panagiotis

Seeds I'd love to see in play, for you to use, bend or ignore. Each says what it rests
on in 2e canon and what it could open. None of them answers one of your mysteries; they
give you levers.

Sources (2e unless marked): *Cormanthyr: Empire of the Elves* (CEoE), *The Fall of Myth
Drannor* (FoMD), *Ruins of Myth Drannor* (RoMD), *Elves of Evermeet* (EoE), *Volo's
Guide to All Things Magical* (VGtATM), *Demihuman Deities* (DD), *Netheril: Empire of
Magic* (NEoM), *Chronomancer*. Mechanics are all 2e; later-edition lore is labelled.

---

## 1. House Dahast already had bladesingers

**Canon.** Around 650 DR the house's Lord Speaker, **Purtham Dahast**, Alea's grandson,
was a gold-elf **bladesinger** (F6/W6). His heir **Lhoris** (CE, F7) had his elder
brothers ambushed and killed to become heir (CEoE p. 113). The house was "nearly
extinct" even then (p. 73).

**Hooks.**
- **Orendyl knew Purtham.** Orendyl's old lodge was in Cormanthor. If he trained
  beside, or under, Purtham, then teaching a Dahast orphan the blade was never chance.
  He just never said so.
- **Lhoris's line.** The elf's mother descends from either the bladesinger or the
  kinslayer. Finding out which is a quiet, personal thread with no world-ending stakes.
  It might be the most painful one he has.

## 2. Alea Dahast's lost selû'kiira

**Canon.**
- **Alea Dahast** was a High Mage from 490 DR, Spell-Major of the Akh'Faer, a caster of
  the 261 DR mythal, and a **friend of the Srinshee** (CEoE pp. 47, 120, 143). She died
  at Oacenth's Second Blooding, Flamerule 714 (FoMD p. 40).
- **A High Mage's selû'kiira** is personal, cut from rainbow tourmaline, and holds both
  magical lore and **the secrets of High Magic**. It should return to the other High
  Mages when its bearer dies. **Some never did:** Grand Mage Ualair's vanished with him
  in 712 (FoMD p. 43; CEoE pp. 152–153).
- **Rules (CEoE pp. 152–153):**
  - Only an elf of Int 15+ can bond with one. A bladesinger already needs Int 15.
  - Acclimation: hourly Int checks, each failure costing 1 Int, with a risk of
    feeblemind.
  - Once bonded it grants ancestral memories, 1d6 spells a year, halved study time and a
    constant *mind blank* against everyone but the gem's own clan.
  - **An unworthy elf is possessed** by the gem's resident presences (*magic jar*, at −5
    to the save), used to carry the gem home, then memory-wiped and released.

**Hooks.**
- **Nobody knows what became of Alea's gem.** She died in battle in the city's last
  week, and the High Mages had their hands full. It could be in the ruins, with someone
  who knew her, or anywhere a High Mage's gem might end up when nobody comes to collect it.
- **A gem that stores memories, for an elf whose memories are in fragments.** I'm not
  suggesting it holds his missing ones. Only that a Dahast heir and a Dahast gem
  meeting would be a scene.
- **The possession clause is a good threat.** An elf found unworthy is used and then
  wiped. His Fragments I–IX would read very differently if he ever learned that is
  something a gem can do.

## 3. Quess'Ar'Teranthvar: the Windsong Tower

**Canon.**
- **The Windsong Tower** (area S16, Sheshyrinnam, the Temple Ward) was a secret
  wizards' school: no visible doors, extradimensional interiors, and proof against
  scrying. Its Elders included **the Srinshee** and Symrustar Auglamyr (CEoE pp. 74–75,
  122, 157–160).
- **The Quess'Ar'Teranthvar**, the Golden Grove of Hidden Knowledge, was **the stolen
  Nether Scrolls**, reshaped by the High Mage **Tyvollus Aluviirsaan** into a golden
  beech with metal leaves. A silver bird and a metal snake live in its branches.
  - It stands in **the Solarium, a pocket dimension** between the tower's top floor and
    its roof.
  - It teaches one student at a time; its sixth mode grants 10th-level spells and High
    Magic rituals.
  - **Its guardian is Tyvollus himself, held in *Adoessuor*, the Reverie of Ages.**
    Disturb him and the tree melts; without him it reverts to scrolls (pp. 158–159).
- **In the ruins (RoMD):** the tower is rubble, and only a **cellar crypt** survives,
  entered by touching a glowing rune floor. A rumour says **magic tore the tower out of
  the ground and flew it away with its surviving mages**.
- (3.5e lore, *Anauroch: The Empire of Shade*: the Shadovar later stole the grove.)

**Hooks.**
- **The Garden beneath Myth Drannor.** Your text: white trees, silver grass, "a sky that
  had no stars", a place that "did not belong beneath Myth Drannor. It did not belong
  anywhere". Canon: a golden tree with metal leaves, in a pocket dimension, inside a
  tower that may have flown away. I'm not saying they're the same. They rhyme, and a
  scholar like Thaelith would have noticed.
- **The cellar crypt is real and reachable**: the one piece of the tower anyone can
  still visit.
- **Tyvollus in stasis outside time**, guarding a tree that teaches. That leads straight
  into §6.

## 4. Elven High Magic: not his to cast, possibly his to carry

**Canon.** In *Cormanthyr*, High Magic is NPC-only: wizard 17+, age 450+, Int and Wis 18,
decades of oral apprenticeship (CEoE p. 127). A 230-year-old fighter/mage can't be a
High Mage. He can be **the heir, the subject or the key** of a ritual. Rituals worth a
look (pp. 132–137):

- **_Akh'Faen'Tel'Quess_** (Solitude): **changes an elf's subrace.** A gold-elf house
  name on a boy drawn to silver: was anything done to him, or to his mother?
- **_Adoessuor_** (Solitude): the Reverie of Ages, a stasis outside time.
- **_Ialyshae'Seldar'Wihylos_** (Complement): the making of kiira **and moonblades**.
- **_N'Quor'Khaor_** (Myriad): **binds a god or fiend, and consumes its casters.** It is
  the ritual that once sealed the Trio Nefarious in their prison (FoMD p. 13).
  - It sounds a great deal like how the **Spectral Legion** sealed Nebiros, and were
    cursed for it (2014 handout).
- **_Uaul'Selu'Keryth_**, the Sundering, which made Evermeet. Only two High Mages know it,
  and **the Srinshee** is one.
- **Mythals.** Myth Drannor's was raised in 261 DR by fourteen casters, Alea among
  them. Its corrupted remnant still runs in the ruins and **dampens fires**, which makes
  "the burning city" of 930 stranger still (RoMD p. 27).

## 5. A blade

Three ways for a blade to enter his story, all with 2e rules.

**a. A Dahast moonblade (if you add one).** Canon gives House Dahast no moonblade.
But 2e moonblades:
- pass down a bloodline;
- add a power for each worthy heir;
- **kill the unworthy** (5d8, or a save vs death for the evil);
- **go dormant when the last bearer of a line is unworthy** (EoE pp. 70–71;
  VGtATM p. 114).

A Dahast blade dormant since **Lhoris** the kinslayer, waiting for a worthy Dahast,
would need no new rules at all. The catch: a bladesinger has one weapon for life, and
the moonblade would have to *be* it.

**b. The Artblade, and the Spell-Major's sword.** The three elfblades of Cormanthyr are
offices, not heirlooms. **Ary'Faern'Kerym, the Artblade** (NG) lets its wielder cast
spells *through the blade* and confers the rank of Spell-Major. **Alea Dahast was
Spell-Major.** After the Artblade was lost in 450 DR, the blade *Faervian* was made in 462
for the Spell-Major (CEoE pp. 154–156; FoMD p. 50).
- Canon names another wielder of *Faervian* by 667, and never says Alea held either
  blade.
- A sword that once answered to his house's greatest mage is still a strong lever.
- The blade-rite tests talent, mind and heart, and is run by High Mages.

**c. Purtham's sword.** The simplest: the blade of the last Dahast bladesinger, in a
ruin or a tomb, or in Orendyl's keeping all along.

## 6. Kernels of chronomancy

Not a plot, just places where time could get in. **From your own text first:**

- The voice in the Garden: "**You are not here.**"
- The woman: "**You are late.**"
- A place that "did not belong anywhere".
- A melody "no living elf has heard for four thousand years".
- The Light as "the first act by which possibility became reality".

**What 2e canon offers:**

- **_Adoessuor_**, the Reverie of Ages: an elf kept outside time, sustained by the Weave
  (CEoE p. 132). Tyvollus is held in it.
- **Mystryl's time conduit**, the only route for time travel in the Realms (CEoE
  pp. 4–7; NEoM pp. 13–14):
  - travellers arrive at New Year and are recalled at year's end, a year older;
  - they can never revisit the same year;
  - they lose their spells and items.
- **Four time gates.** One is **in Synnoria in the Moonshaes, known only to elven High
  Mages**: an arch in a misty cave behind the eastern falls of Mirror Lake (CEoE p. 6).
- **Labelas Enoreth**, the elven god of time (DD pp. 143–145):
  - his specialty priests are **chronologians**;
  - his **Knights Paradoxical** guard the time gates and hunt copies of the *time
    conduit* spell.
- **The *Chronomancer* sourcebook** (2e) allows **elven chronomancers** (to 15th level).
  Its Realms appendix says that **agents of Oghma and Deneir can undo a chronomancer's
  changes** (pp. 4–5, 88).
  - That's a direct bridge to my Oghmanite, whose deepest fear is something erased so
    completely that nobody remembers it existed.
  - Ruan Famkul means "that which has been **deliberately removed from remembrance**".
  - I'm not proposing an answer. Only noting that canon puts Oghma's servants on exactly
    that front line.

### 1.2 Settled facts that weren't on paper before

- **The Blood Moon is 1160 DR** [T, MAL]: Khelben's gloss on "When moon is bled" is "The Blood
  Moon phenomenon of 1160 DR". This year. That closes half of web gap 11.
- **Netheril's fall and Xinlenal**, "first enclave", are Alaundo's "first crown shattered in dust"
  (MAL). [C] Canon calls Xinlenal Netheril's first **floating city**, made by Ioulaum in −2954 DR,
  and it fell with Karsus's Folly in −339 (*Lost Empires of Faerûn*).
- **The Candlekeep letter is signed *U.*** (CK), and it is sealed as Candlekeep's: a red wax
  tower in a ring (its one image). [C] Canon's Keeper of the Tomes at Candlekeep in 1368–1372 is
  **Ulraunt** (FRCS 3e). That's two centuries late for 1160, but Panagiotis has borrowed
  later canon names before (Jazrac, web §14). A guess, *weak*.
- **Pylacheus has a first name.** Khelben: *"His name was Aemund. They called him 'the Tired',
  because he had lived long enough to have the right to be tired. The name he remained known by,
  of course, was Pylacheus the Heretic."* (APO)
- **The medallion came from Dumathoin's country.** Khelben got it on "a journey beyond the planes
  that most sane people don't often attempt", and says the PCs know "the so-called **Outlands**,
  as well as the place your circles call **Dwarven Mountain**". At first glance it bears "some
  names, and runes you don't recognise" (WAR). [C] In 2e the **Dwarvish Mountain** is shared by
  three dwarven powers, and one of its halls, **Deepshaft Hall**, is **Dumathoin's** (*Demihuman
  Deities*). The medallion came out of Temag's own god's house.
- **The auction.** Khelben stepped in: the swords "were used as a small part of the payment for
  gathering information and answers. That will also be your compensation… As for the House, don't
  worry: it has been settled financially." (AUC) The swords bought what he then told you.

### 2.1 Pylacheus speaks (APO)

Khelben reads two documents in a row. **Thalassar**, "a man we don't know for certain ever
existed… some refuse even the name, as if saying it brings something closer", writes on the three
lines (*For what is more / Forever or never more / Never or forevermore*): "two kinds of darkness",
and the moment when "one person, perhaps, will stand before the seals and decide… with this
question alive in him… And if you are the one who stands there… I hope you already know your
answer."

Then **Aemund, that is Pylacheus**, answers him: *"Brother Thalassar."* The letter is the
gentlest text in the pack. *For what is more*: the answer exists but can only be lived, "in the
hand that reaches for a stranger… in the person who gets up again". *Forever*: not a sentence,
"the acceptance that what exists… deserves to go on, because in it there is still the possibility
of change". He grants Thalassar half the way on *Never*: injustice, the indifference of the
powerful, "the silence of good people before evil" should be the Never. And then:

> *"Between 'Never' and 'Forevermore' there is a gap. And in that gap live people… Ilmater doesn't
> ask us to win. He asks us not to abandon that gap."* Signed: *"The servant of Ilmater who loves
> you despite everything, Aemund."*

Khelben's frame: *"one of the very few who faced such 'knowledge' and did not bow. Perhaps that's
how he got the epithet."*

Why this is the most important page for Ancelm: §3.3.

### 2.2 The seven seals have a structure (APO)

- Seals 1–4: the Four Horsemen. The handout gives them an **Ilmatari** strand: "especially in the
  Church of Ilmater", the world's trials were described, among other ways, as four horsemen
  galloping after the Darkness, or the Light. That usage is so old it's no longer even mentioned,
  because of "the Temple and the **Divine Order**" that forbade parts of this history.
- Seal 5 releases "the **martyred cries** of the world toward the gods (terrifying amounts of
  energy)".
- Seal 6 brings cataclysms "with the **diffusion of the Elements** into the world".
- Seal 7 calls seven **Trumpet Archons** with seven vials of plague and wrath "(or the opposite,
  the 7 aspects???)". The question marks are the DM's. [C] Trumpet archons are canon (2e
  *Monstrous Manual*, "Archon").
- *"Behold the Hand of God who takes away the sins of the world"*, with the handout's own note:
  **God, not Gods**. Opening the scroll goes with the second Reckoning, "the Temple's people say
  the second coming of Nebiros". The Chosen opens the first four.
- Khelben, before he reads any of it: some of this is "at the discretion of the gods… forbidden
  and hidden by their own order"; whether it gets said "is their decision". And: *"I can surmise
  with some safety that you have exceeded the limits of simple mortal existence, and I am willing
  to entertain that hypothesis."*

**The valley Khelben is sending Temag to is the valley on Dahast's door.** I'd put this at the top
of anything you say to Panagiotis about how the characters were tied together. It landed.

### 3.2 Hide the glyph — *strong as a question, medium as an answer*

Moradin, at the end of the forging: ***"Flame's Bane will hold, if the blood remembers. Hide the
glyph. Seal the secret. Let none unworthy temper the fire again."*** (WAR)

Set beside it:

- Dahast's mark is **dwarven** [S Sun 0:22], found only in a dwarven record of the closing
  (THA), and **carved on the closing door** (ELF).
- Its name is now an order: **that which must be made forgotten** (THA).
- Khelben: the knowledge of how the Wards were made "**has not been erased. It has been
  sealed.** Hidden. Protected. Locked away" (WAR).
- Thaelith: *"I do not believe it is a mark of his blood"*, *"a mark of something that happened
  before he had a name"*; and *"do not assume that what was hidden there was hidden because it
  was evil."* (THA)
- No name means no page in Jergal's book, "between the lines, where the pen cannot reach, or
  will not" [S Sun 2:04–2:08].

[I] **The mark may be the glyph Moradin ordered hidden**, and the hiding place a newborn who
would never have a name: outside the book, outside prophecy, the one place the pen can't reach.
If so, "came from the west, though no elf had crossed the passes" is the elves who came to carry
it away.

[I] **Then the lock has two halves, and the party holds both.** Flame's Bane "will hold, **if the
blood remembers**": that's Temag, the Forgemaster's heir ("You carry inheritance"), with a sigil
on his chest and the sliver in his hand. And the glyph, "**not of his blood**": that's Dahast. One
inheritance by blood, one placed on a body that had no name to place it under. Two dwarven
sigils on two bodies, one fleeting and one permanent.

Against: nothing in the text says the mark is *the* glyph, and "hide the glyph" may mean a rune
on the ward itself. Ask it at the table as a question, through Temag or Dahast; it's a deduction
either character can make from what he heard.

[I, medium] Aemund's letter is the first text in Pylacheus's own voice, and it sounds like the
mentor's Pylacheus, not the record's. Three readings, all live:

1. **Redactor forgery.** The Handbook was written or altered by people who "forge, remove and burn

2. **His own decoy.** The mentor's thesis taken literally: Pylacheus made himself unreliable on
   purpose, and the Handbook is part of the false trail. The trail then fell into the wrong hands

The deaths don't agree either: replaced at his execution, dead of illness, or dead of his own
practice. Two of those can't both be true. Ancelm's kit powers, *Detect Deception/Illusion* and
*Dispel Falsehood*, are built to tell a forged page from a true one (KIT, and the questions file
#17–18).

[I, weak] **The monk in Ancelm's dream may be Aemund.** He is "aged. **Tired.** But calm"
(WGV), and Aemund's byname is "**the Tired**" (APO). He's an Ilmatari in Selimbrar's abbey,

And the man the copyist hunts *"knows them by heart… uses them for something Selimbrar would
never recognise. Pylacheus would."* The newer sheet answers *"The saint suffered willingly"* with

### 3.4 Alaundo's ending is Selimbrar's test — *medium*

The full last lines: *"And only the hand that suffers willingly, / And only the blade that bleeds
for another, / May turn aside the storm."* (MAL)

- "Suffers willingly" is word for word the **first** of Selimbrar's Seven Signs: *"First: it is
  willing. No one forced you."* (SEL leaf 3)
- "Bleeds for another" is Selimbrar's last-night distinction: the man who stands in the fire *to
  save another*, against the one who stands there because he likes to burn (SEL leaf 5).
- The Bhaalist answers the same doctrine with *"So do I"* [S Sat 0:31].

[I] So the prophecy names **Selimbrar's** sufferer, and the campaign already contains a
counterfeit of one. NOTES theory 5 read the lines as Ancelm and Dahast. This sharpens it: the
test isn't whether you suffer, it's **for what, and whether you could stop**, which are rules 4
and 5, the two the Handbook removed.

Two smaller readings, *weak*:

- "Mark the Black Dunes, **O children of dawn**": Lathander's (Gen Soleilon was a Dawnknight,
  WG), or the elves' **Keepers of First Dawn** (ELF). The prophecy doesn't say.
- "The storm": in the Candlekeep letter the Temple is the storm and Bhaal "the dagger after",
  "a dagger in its center" (CK). In Alaundo the storm is the Bone Gale, a dracolich carrying
  Bhaal's shard. A storm with a dagger in it, twice.

### 3.5 The Temple is early — *arithmetic on two [T] dates*

CK: the Temple "reappears about every thousand years, each time with a similar pattern". Its two
dated episodes: **−598 DR** and **400 DR**, 998 years apart. The next would fall about **1400**.
The DM says it's active now [S Sun 2:28], in **1160**: about **240 years early**.

The cycle is "about", and the letter notes earlier episodes with no Bhaal in them, so this proves
nothing on its own. But it fits what Khelben said about Bhaal's return, unprompted, from the
other side: *"Gods are many things. Subtle… is not usually one of them. This was… arranged."*
(AUC) **Something is running ahead of its clock.** The Blood Moon came first, then Bhaal came back
assembled, then the nodes started stirring.

- Birakar is Lord of the Timekeeper Hall of Tethyamar [S Sat 10:31]: the time-keeper.

[I] The campaign's memory-keepers are the dwarves, and its one deliberate forgetting is also

### 3.12 The endgame has a script — *strong, as a source decode*

The 2014 Hedrack journal is *Return to the Temple of Elemental Evil*'s handout with the names
swapped (web §15), and the Black Cyst came up at the table [S Sun 1:13]. [C] RttToEE's cult has a

the **Orb of Oblivion**, so the gates work again. Have the **Champion of Elemental Evil** call the
four Princes of Elemental Evil into their **nodes**. Bring them to the Greater Temple, where the
Orb of Oblivion infuses **Tharizdun** with enough power to escape his extradimensional prison.
When the module opens, **only the Fire Node is fully accessible**: the fire gem is already in the
Orb, and Imix, Prince of Evil Fire, is in the node. The Champion is **Lareth the Beautiful**. And
in the Black Cyst, a voice: *"The Orb of Oblivion has already been taken to prepare for **my
second coming**."*

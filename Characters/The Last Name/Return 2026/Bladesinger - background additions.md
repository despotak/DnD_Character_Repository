## 1. → after THE CHILD

**LITTLE STAR:** Nobody remembers who first called you *Little Star*. Saelith says it was
the other children, because you would never go to sleep before the stars came out. You
would lie on your back in the grass above the halls and refuse to come in until you had
counted them, and you never finished, and you were never satisfied.

In Sarenestar Ael'Vala, children are named in their first spring, under the open sky, by
whoever loves them most. Every spring you watched other children named. Every spring
you stood at the edge of the circle. Nobody was unkind about it. That was almost worse.

## 2. → after THE FOREST OF MIR (the community)

**NOT TO BE FOUND:** Your people lived the way their saying said they did.

No fires after dusk. No paths worn into the ground; you were taught to walk so that the
grass stood up again behind you. The real homes were below: halls and storerooms dug
under the roots, linked by tunnels that the elders said ran further than anyone had
mapped. Above ground there was only forest.

You learned early that the most important skill in the world is silence, and the second
most important is knowing when to break it.

Other elves lived in Mir, many of them, in the great tribes to the west. Your people had
as little to do with them as with the humans of Calimshan. [Panagiotis: how does the
community relate to the other elves of Mir? See Questions.]

## 3. → after THE FIRST SWORD

**ORENDYL'S LODGE:** Orendyl had not always lived in Mir. In his youth he had belonged to
a lodge of bladesingers [in Cormanthor before the fall / on Evermeet], where every member
mastered one weapon and kept it for life. He never told you why he left, and you learned
not to ask.

The first thing he taught you was not the sword. It was **dance**. For two years you
danced: the circle dances of the community, the slow ones and the fast ones, until your
feet stopped needing your attention. Only then did he hand you the wooden sword.

"A bladesinger who can't dance," he said, "is a swordsman who sings badly."

## 4. → after THE FIRST BLADESONG

**THE GREAT CAT:** You were [twenty-five] when Orendyl decided you were ready to choose your
weapon. There was no ceremony, because there was no lodge. He simply put three blades on
a table and left you alone with them overnight.

In the morning you had chosen the oldest of the three: a mithral long sword whose guard
was carved as oak leaves closing around a crescent moon. When you drew it, it chimed. He
nodded, as if you had confirmed something. It was **Rathael**, the Moon Between Blades,
which answers only to Dahast blood. [How it came to be on Orendyl's table is
Panagiotis's to say.]

"Then this is your weapon until you die," he said. "Every other blade will feel wrong in
your hand. That is the price."

That evening he tattooed the mark of his old lodge on your shoulder in the old way: a
great cat, for the long sword. It took all night. You didn't make a sound, and he said
that was the second-best thing you had ever done.

**THE OATH:** Before he'd teach you anything more, he made you swear the bladesinger's
oath: that you would come to the aid of any elf in need, at any cost, unless that elf
had proven themselves an enemy.

You swore it easily. You were young. It didn't occur to you then how hard it can be to
know who has proven what.

## 5. → after THE LONG NIGHT and THE GARDEN DISAPPEARS

**FIGHTING IN THE DARK:** The Long Night lasted almost two years. You spent much of it
training with your eyes closed. There was little point keeping them open.

Orendyl put a bell on his own ankle, then took it off. By the end of the first winter
you could fight him by the sound of his breathing alone. By the end of the second you
could fight him when he wasn't breathing at all.

You didn't know then why it would matter. Then you went down beneath Myth Drannor.

**THE EXPEDITION:** You went to Myth Drannor as part of a larger expedition, because it
suited you. Following the star was your own decision, and you went alone. Of everyone who
went down beneath the city, only two came back alive: you, and the expedition's leader.
[Who the others were, and who led them, is Panagiotis's to name.]

The leader saw that you had come back changed. Apart from him, very few know.

[Whether Orendyl's elven chain came to you then or the day you left Mir (§9) is open.]

## 6. → after THE YEARS OF SEARCHING

**THE ROADS:** From 1152 onward you travelled more than you had in the two centuries
before. Some of the places you went:

- **The forest of Tethir**, where the elves called the Elmanesse live. They descend from
  those who fled Myth Drannor's fall, and you hoped someone there would remember House
  Dahast. One old woman did. She said the house had died before the city did, and that
  its last heirs had not loved each other. She would not say more.
- **The edge of the old elven woods** around Myth Drannor, and further in than you
  should have gone, more than once.
- **Hidden places in Mir itself**: ruins older than your community, and [a mythal-town
  that does not wish to be named].
- [Candlekeep, with Thaelith, for one season. She searched; you mostly waited at the
  gate, because the price of entry was a book and you had none to give.]

Each time you asked the same three questions. *What is this mark? Who was my mother?
What is the Light?* Each time you came home with more questions than you left with.

**HOUSE DAHAST:** [Option, from canon.] Somewhere on those roads you learned the names of
the last of your house: a patriarch, **Purtham**, and his heir, **Lhoris**, who was
said to have murdered his own elder brothers to become heir. You don't know which of
them your mother descended from. Some nights you hope it was Purtham. Some nights you
are afraid to find out.

## 7. → after THAELITH'S LAST LETTER

**WHY KHELBEN:** You asked her once, before the end, why Khelben Arunsun of all people.
A human mage in a human city.

She was quiet a long time. Then she said:

"He was born in Myth Drannor, in the days of its glory. And by the custom of that
time, he was given no name."

You stared at her.

"He went out into the world without one. He fought for the city as a nameless man. He
found his name afterwards, and it cost him dearly." She looked at your covered shoulder,
where the mark is. "He knows what it is to have nothing where a name should be. That is
why."

It was the only time she ever told you something she knew for certain about your
mystery's edges. You wrote it down that night, word for word.

## 8. → after THE LAST MEMORY, Fragments I–IX

**AFTERWARDS:** You don't remember the road home from the dead elf's house. You remember
arriving.

Since then you catch yourself **humming two notes** and then stopping, because you know
there should be a third, and its absence feels like a missing step on a stair in the
dark.

And you think about the oath you swore Orendyl. *Any elf in need, unless proven an
enemy.* She spoke like an elf. You couldn't kill her. You don't know whether she is an
enemy, and you don't know whether you are still bound to her by whatever you agreed to.

You have not told anyone about the oath and the woman in the same breath. It feels like
saying something out loud would decide it.

## 9. → THE DECISION (leaving Mir)

**LEAVING:** You left in the spring.

Saelith, very old now, came to the edge of the forest with you. She was the one who had
said *"Then he has no name"* two hundred and thirty years before. She didn't apologise
for it. She said: "We gave you nothing to carry but yourself. It seems you found plenty
to carry anyway." Then she kissed your forehead and went back into the trees without
looking round. That is how your people say goodbye.

[If Orendyl did not vanish:] Orendyl gave you his **elven chain**. It was old, finer
than anything made now, and it fit you as if it had been waiting. "It has outlived three
owners," he said. "Try to make it four."

Then you walked north out of the forest, onto the human roads, with a sealed letter, a
silver leaf, a white seed, a sword you will carry until you die, and a mark you have
never let anyone draw.

## For Panagiotis: the "burning city" of 930

Canon has no fire at Myth Drannor in 930. More than that: the ruins' surviving mythal
**dampens forest fires** (*Ruins of Myth Drannor* p. 27). So if something burned there in
930, it burned *through* the mythal. Some options, only if useful:

- **The ruins themselves**: something woke beneath the city and the mythal failed to
  stop it.
- **A town on the edge of the old elven woods**: Elventree was founded in 720 for
  trade with the outside world.
- **The Trio Nefarious**, if in our world they survived the Weeping War.

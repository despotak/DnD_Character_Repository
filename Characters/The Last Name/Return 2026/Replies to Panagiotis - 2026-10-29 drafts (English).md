## Reply 1

**To:** Panayotis Galanopoulos
**Subject:** Re: ΕΡΩΤΗΣΗ ΓΙΑ ΤΟΝ Mr.DAHAST

> My dear DM,
>
> Late, but here it is: the elf's months in Waterdeep. First, one question back: did Khelben see
> me at all in those months, or did I only get as far as the Tower's door? Either works. I never
> opened Thaelith's letter. I was tempted more than once, and I didn't.
>
> What I'd have done while I waited:
>
> 1. **The sixth rune.** Your Rathael text says I *recently* deciphered the Rune of the White
>    Tree. I'd like the Waterdeep months to be when that happened, if it fits how the sword came
>    to me. After that, the riddle: "the unfallen tear of Sehanine's Daughter", "the silver-leaf
>    root". I'd ask Selûne's clergy at the House of the Moon, and any elf in the city who keeps
>    Sehanine's rites, what "Sehanine's Daughter" could mean. And since Rathael answers only to Dahast
>    blood: what a blade bound to one bloodline does in the wrong hand, and whether it sleeps when
>    its line goes quiet.
> 2. **Delzoun, quietly.** Dwarven sages, smiths and temples: the last days of Delzoun, an old
>    northern route where the dwarven roads go under the mountains, frozen anvils, and any story
>    of elves up there. The nearest dwarven memory is under my feet: the Melairkyn dug beneath Mount
>    Waterdeep long ago, with the leave of the elves of Aelinthaldaar (see 7), and then vanished. A
>    long-sealed temple of Dumathoin, the Vault of Hidden Silences, is said to keep their crypts
>    still. Elves and dwarves once shared this mountain, and I'd want to know what they kept
>    together. Through sages only; I'd stay out of the deep halls. I'd never show the mark, since Saelith forbade copying it and I've never
>    drawn it. I wouldn't say *Ruan Famkul* to strangers either. A mark that means "removed from
>    remembrance" teaches you to ask around the edges.
> 3. **The Blood Moon.** Who else saw anything that night: temples, astrologers, sailors at the
>    docks. Did anyone else have visions, or was it only me?
> 4. **House Dahast.** Elves or half-elves in Waterdeep with Myth Drannor blood, any record of the
>    Dahasts after 714, any trace of my mother, Elyndra. Whether my house ever had High
>    Mages, and what became of them and of their gems: a High Mage's *selû'kiira* is meant to go
>    back to the others when its bearer dies, and some never did. A gem that holds a house's
>    memories, for an elf whose own come in fragments. And would I have heard any of the old
>    stories about Khelben himself, and whether he has any tie to Myth Drannor?
> 5. **The Song.** I'd hum the two notes I remember to every elven bard and minstrel who'd stand
>    still for it, in case one of them knows the third.
> 6. **Names.** This is the one I couldn't leave alone. Every elf gets a secret name from the
>    parents and the priest, and a public one for everyone else. I have neither, and Thaelith said
>    something else had written itself where mine should be. So I'd go after what a true name *is*.
>    The elves' own answer is gentle: knowing an elf's true name gives no power over him, and telling
>    it is a gift of trust. The old High Magic is less gentle: its apprentices learned the true names
>    of animals and folk, and a High Mage could speak a god's true name in ritual and cut its avatar
>    from the Weave. Somewhere between those two answers is mine. Can a name be written over? Taken?
>    And if something wrote itself in the place of mine, is that a name, or the opposite of one? The
>    elven chronicles even have a Year of the True Names, 464: the year a nameless human called
>    Arun's Son died in Anauroch saving three Evereskan elves, and Mystra brought him back as her
>    Chosen. The Nameless Chosen. How a man with no name comes by one is a question I'd carry a
>    long way. I'd never ask anyone for their name, and never tell anyone I lack one.
> 7. **Before the Folly.** Mystra's Weave is younger than the Art: before Karsus it was Mystryl's,
>    and before the Netherese found the Nether Scrolls in the ruins of Aryvandaar, the magic they
>    knew was what the elves had taught them. I'd want the elven magic under all of it: High Magic
>    before the mythals, the Crown Wars, the Dark Disaster that turned Miyeritar into the High Moor.
>    It's the history elves rarely speak of even among themselves, which is exactly why I'd want it.
>    And it's under my feet. Waterdeep stands where Aelinthaldaar stood, Illefarn's capital, razed
>    with High Magic on its own coronal's order before its people sailed for Evermeet. No ruins were
>    left. Only crypts, and a high magic that still runs, a secret the city's own elves keep. I'd try
>    to earn my way to them, and ask, as one elf to another, what was worth unmaking a city rather
>    than leaving it behind. It's my mark's question in stone: forgotten by design.
> 8. **Where things begin, carefully.** The one I'd keep closest. I'd never say the words Garden,
>    Seed or Light aloud. The sword is my cover: Rathael wants "the unfallen tear of Sehanine's
>    Daughter", and the elves say they sprang from Corellon's blood mingled with Sehanine's tears. A
>    bladesinger asking about that is pious, not suspicious. From there, slowly, the rest: how the
>    elves tell the making of the world, and how the House of the Moon tells it, Selûne's light and
>    Shar's dark, with Mystryl born of their battle. The first light and the first night. What the
>    Moon's priests made of the Long Night, which everyone lived through, so asking costs nothing.
>    And, only ever in passing, whether any telling, elven or older, has a light before the moon, or
>    a song before the singer. Bards for the songs, priests for the stories, sages for the texts;
>    never the same person twice, never the whole question at once. If anyone asked why: the sword.
>    It's even true.
> 9. **Ardeep.** Less than a day's ride from the walls is the last of an elven kingdom, and in it
>    the place most sacred to Sehanine: the Tears of Aloevan, reached through a pool in a glen that
>    doesn't quite belong to the forest around it. Aloevan was a moon-elf queen, Chosen of both
>    Sehanine and Mystra, who couldn't hold the silver fire inside her. When she died, her spirit
>    was caught between this world and Arvandor, and Sehanine's priests rebuilt her court inside
>    that fire. The way in opens only when the sun is eclipsed. I'd go with my riddle about
>    Sehanine's tear, which is reason enough for any priest of hers. And I'd ask them about the
>    Veiled Choir: Sehanine's seers, who sing what they see, in temples even the elves have
>    forgotten. No one finds them by following someone else's steps, only by looking for
>    themselves. I've been looking for a song my whole life.
> 10. **The book and the blade.** Bladesong practice every day, and work on my spellbook. I've
>    ranked the spells I'd ask to learn. Two rules questions decide how far that goes: do you use
>    the optional limit on spells per level, and does the starting book need learn rolls? And
>    which books beyond the PHB do you allow?
>
> And, sentimentally: on clear nights I'd look up, because of the seed. "When you see the sky
> without stars…"
>
> Whatever I'd have found, or failed to find, is yours to decide. I'd rather some of it was dead
> ends.
>
> Also, thank you for the medallion! Is it the silver pendant with "the old Dahast emblem", the
> leaf from the foot of the Dahast arms? If so, that settles my question about the pendant. And is
> "When the ember remembers itself, follow the Song" on its back?
>
> Chris

> ### 3. Theories
>
> Take these as the characters thinking aloud.
>
> - **One valley.** The Shrine of Moradin's Flame is in the valley on Dahast's door. Temag carries
>   the blood that "remembers"; Dahast carries a dwarven mark that Thaelith says is *not* of his
>   blood. Two halves of something.
> - **Pylacheus's record has been tampered with.** He has three portraits: the heretic of the
>   leaves, the gentle man of the letter, and the scholar my mentor thought was hiding in plain
>   sight. And he has three ends: condemned and saved when Gestas took his place (as our notes
>   have it), dead of illness, or dead of his own practice. They can't all be true. Somebody wanted us to believe one version, and
>   Anselm wants to know who.
> - **Alaundo's last lines are Selimbrar's test.** "The hand that suffers willingly" is the first
>   of the Seven Signs, and "the blade that bleeds for another" is Selimbrar's man who stands in
>   the fire to save someone else. The prophecy names the true sufferer, and the abbey already
>   showed us a counterfeit: the newer sheet that answered that it, too, suffers willingly, and the
>   only difference is the God it names. And it isn't the
>   only hand in the pack. The Hand of God is the Chosen who opens the first seals; the "hand
>   unclean" is Red Handed; Ilmater's symbol is two bound hands. Khelben said seals are broken by
>   love, not by the wicked. Anselm's fear: whoever could open them and whoever could turn the storm
>   aside are the same person, told apart only by the choice.
> - **The Temple is early.** The Candlekeep letter has it returning about every thousand years,
>   at −598 and 400 DR. The next would be around 1400, and it's 1160. Khelben said Bhaal's return
>   was arranged. So, perhaps, is the Temple's. And both signs before it were ours: the Long
>   Night came when the old party went into the Demiplane of Shadow for Matt's memories, and Anselm
>   read the rain of bodies after Karran-Kul as a sign that Birakar and Temag had touched Bhaal's
>   return, or its prevention. Did we do this?
> - **The seals are the nodes.** A second Reckoning needs a first, and Khelben's chronicle has one:
>   four unbound elemental powers, and Moradin's answer. Khelben reads "the fourth part of the
>   World" as perhaps the four elements, and the sixth seal looses them. So the first four seals may
>   be the nodes, the Chosen who opens them the Temple's Champion, and the Wards the dwarves' seals
>   over them.
> - **The fifth seal runs on false martyrs.** It releases the martyrs' cries of the world to the
>   gods. Selimbrar's seventh sign says true martyrdom asks no recognition, not even from the god,
>   and Pylacheus cut the fourth and fifth, measure and an end. Endless pain crying out to be seen
>   is the fuel. And a μάρτυρας is also a witness: those who remember burn too.
> - **Someone is counting names.** "When the last name is spoken, I shall take the breath." And the
>   crypt's carving says the one who ends all things awaits the last breath. Jergal was once the
>   Lord of the End of Everything; his
>   priests read the year's dead aloud and, at the last name, cried "one year closer". Anselm
>   dreamt THE DEAD HAVE BEEN READ. And Jergal ends a life by writing its name: is that why Hedrack,
>   who has none, keeps coming back?

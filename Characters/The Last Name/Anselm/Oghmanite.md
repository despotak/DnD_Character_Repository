# The Oghmanite (secondary character)

Human, servant of **Oghma**, a **Shield of the Scroll** of the *Children of the
Passive Voice*. Hunts the Redactor **Corvus**. Carries half of a dead mentor's riddle,
the names of nine forgotten beings, and a word he can't remember. In Westgate for the
last two years on Harper business.

**Decided (2026-10-08):** Panagiotis's custom kit, the Shield of the Scroll.
**Inspiration:** mainly Paulo Coelho's *Manual of the Warrior of Light*; secondarily
Umberto Eco's *The Name of the Rose* and Arturo Pérez-Reverte's *The Club Dumas*.
**Name:** none yet. (Nadari's account in the Westgate document calls him "Theodmund";
whether that was meant as his name is Questions #35.)

Sources: Panagiotis's `OGHMANITE BACKGROUND` and `Custom Kit- The Shield of the
Scroll`. Everything marked **[proposed]** is my addition.

## 1. Who he is, in five lines

- **"According to whom?"** His reflex. The surviving version of a story is never
  assumed to be the whole of it.
- **He hears when a word is wrong.** The *Missing Definition*: "Something is being
  called something it is not." Sometimes he's wrong, and he knows that too.
- **He chose the book over the man.** He let Corvus go to pull the Ledger of Grace out
  of the fire, with his hands. He called it a victory, and was "not entirely convinced".
- **He can't lie, and won't destroy.** Both are vows (KIT). Silence and precision are
  his only defences.
- **He's afraid of one thing above all:** that something could be erased so completely
  "that nobody even remembers that there was ever anything to search for".

## 2. How he plays at the table

**[proposed]**, built from the text:

- **Speech.** Precise. Corrects terms, including other people's ("That isn't a
  ritual, it's a binding"). Under the Vow of Truth, when he can't say something he says
  "I won't answer that", never a lie. Asks who wrote a thing before what it says.
- **Body.** **Both hands scarred** from reaching into the ritual fire at the Ledger of
  Grace. A **thigh wound** from Corvus's sacrificial dagger at Asbravn; it healed, but
  it aches in the cold and he favours it when tired.
- **Kit.** The Weighted Pack, 15 lb he can't put down (KIT): reference books, inks,
  parchment, a journal. **[proposed]** He writes everything down the same night, then
  copies the most important pages a second time and sends the copy somewhere else.
  That's the Living Testament in practice: never one copy.
- **Method: the passive voice.** His order's name is a discipline. "A page was removed"
  comes before "Corvus removed the page": he describes the deed first and names no doer
  until the evidence does. The Redactors use the same grammar as a weapon, erasing the
  doer altogether. (The idea comes from *The Name of the Rose*, where some crimes turn out
  to have no single author.)
- **Method: certain errors.** He keeps a ledger of hypotheses and **crosses out** the
  dead ones, never scraping them off, because his vow forbids destroying even his own
  pages. His false patterns stay on the record, which keeps him honest. (See the
  background additions, §5.)
- **Habit.** Reads the margins first. Marginalia tell you who owned a book; the text
  only tells you who wrote it.
- **Flaw.** Can't leave a mystery alone: a locked door, a damaged inscription, a
  missing page. "You know that you will probably do it again."
- **Flaw.** Asbravn. "Someone else had been investigating **you**." He checks who's
  watching before he checks what's on the shelf.
- **What drives him now.** Orianna was murdered a year ago, and she was his friend
  before she was his contact. He stayed in Westgate after the mission was "complete" for
  a reason he hasn't fully said aloud.

## 5. Rules and build

AD&D 2nd Edition. Sources: *Player's Handbook* 1995 (PHB), *The Complete Paladin's
Handbook* (CPaH), *Faiths & Avatars* (F&A), *The Complete Bard's Handbook* (CBH),
*Forgotten Realms Adventures* (FRA).

### First, a word about "lorekeeper"

In canon, **"Lorekeepers" is the name of Oghma's specialty priests** (F&A p. 133), and
"**loremaster**" is the title every priest of Oghma carries (p. 132). Panagiotis's kit
borrows the second word, and I've been using the first as a nickname. Worth saying
plainly to him that I mean the Shield of the Scroll, unless we pick the specialty
priest on purpose (option C below).

### What canon confirms

- **The Children of the Passive Voice are canon**: "an order of learned monks whose
  members protect many libraries and abbeys". The line Panagiotis quotes is verbatim:
  "While the church of Oghma sponsors no military or knightly orders, it spreads its
  aegis over a countless number of monkish fellowships, scholarly orders …" (F&A
  p. 133). The same page adds that "the Oghmanyte faith also has ties with Those Who
  Harp (the Harpers)", which is a canon basis for his Harper friendships.
- The novice's charge to Oghma's clergy includes "**Curb and deny falsehood, rumor, and
  deceitful accounts and histories whenever you encounter them**" and "Never deliver a
  message falsely or incompletely" (F&A p. 132). That is the doctrine the Vow of Truth
  and *Dispel Falsehood* rest on.
- Oghma is **neutral**; his clergy are LN, N or CN (F&A pp. 131–132). His avatar wields a
  *long sword +3*. F&A gives no "favoured weapon" field; the longsword as Oghma's weapon
  comes from 3e.

### The four ways to build him

**Decided: A, Panagiotis's kit.** It is the character the background describes:
a sword-carrying investigator, which neither B nor C is. *Dispel Falsehood* is the
power that ties him to the elf. And it has good precedent. The closest official
equivalent is CPaH's **Inquisitor** (p. 57), "a scholar as well as a warrior" who hunts
users of evil magic. It drops the same four powers (lay on hands, cure disease, turning
and, in its case, spells) and gains anti-illusion and anti-mind-control powers
instead. The added Charm sphere has a precedent in the **Divinate** kit (CPaH p. 48).

**The one change I'd ask for: some healing.** Standard paladins have the Healing
sphere, and so do Realms paladins (F&A p. 183: "combat, divination, healing, law, and
protection"). The kit trades Healing for All and Charm. With Birakar a fighter/thief and
the elf a fighter/mage, **nobody in the party heals**. Either Healing at 9th level, or a
small lay on hands, would fix that without touching the kit's identity.

### Standard paladin strictures the kit doesn't mention

The kit replaces some paladin rules and is silent on others. These need a yes or no:

- **10 magic items** (your Word comment). PHB p. 40, verbatim: "A paladin may not
  possess more than 10 magical items. Furthermore, these may not exceed one suit of
  armor, one shield, four weapons (arrows and bolts are not counted), and four other
  magical items." CPaH p. 26: "Under no circumstances… regardless of his level, kit, or
  status."
- **The 10% tithe** "to a lawful good institution", and lawful-good-only hirelings and
  henchmen. The kit's *Tithe of Lore* replaces the wealth rule, but these two are
  written for lawful good.
- **The fall.** PHB triggers are a *chaotic* act (penance) and an *evil* act (permanent
  loss). For a neutral Shield, what replaces them, beyond lying and destroying a book?
- **The holy sword.** A paladin with a holy sword projects a circle that dispels hostile
  magic. Does a Shield of the Scroll get that, and against whom?

### How *Dispel Falsehood* plays, if it uses the turning table

The kit says it uses "the standard Paladin Turning table against the spell caster's
effective level". Paladins turn as clerics two levels lower (PHB). Reading the
caster's level as the undead row of PHB Table 61:

- **At 5th level** (turning as cleric 3): needs 4+ against a 1st-level caster, 10 against
  2nd, 13 against 3rd–4th, 16 against 5th, 20 against 6th. Nothing against 7th or higher.
- **At 9th level** (cleric 7): automatic against 1st–4th; 4, 10, 13, 16, 19, 20 against
  5th–10th. **Nothing against 11th and above.**

So whatever erased the elf's memories, if it was a powerful caster, the Shield can't
undo it until he's very high level, if ever. That may be exactly right for the story,
but it's worth agreeing up front.

### *Legend Lore* "as a bard"

The PHB bard has no ability with that name. It has a 5% per level chance to identify
"the general purpose and function of any magical item". The **CBH True Bard's Legend
Lore** (p. 17) is the item version, with optional rules for more detail. The kit's text
also covers "ancient ruins, or legendary figures", which is closer to the 1e bard's
legend lore (people, places and things). Which one?

Note: the CBH **Loremaster** bard kit (p. 40) requires **Int 14 and Wis 14**, the same
as Panagiotis's kit. Possibly where the name came from.

### [proposed] Proficiencies

- **Kit bonus:** Reading/Writing, Ancient History, one Ancient Language.
- **Recommended by the kit, and all in the story:** Ancient Languages (more than one),
  Religion, Heraldry (bloodlines), Spellcraft, Local History.
- **From the background:** **Forgery** used the other way round (spotting
  alterations), Appraising (manuscripts), Languages (Thorass, Dethek runes), Etiquette
  (temples, abbeys), and Riding for the destrier.

## 6. A name

He has none yet. A shortlist, each with its reason. None of these names appears
anywhere in Panagiotis's documents, so there's no clash with an NPC.

| Name | Meaning | Why it fits him | Nods to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anselm** | Old High German *ans* "god" + *helm* "helmet, protection": **the god's helm** | A shield's name. And the medieval **Anselm of Canterbury** gave the Oghmanite creed its three words: *fides quaerens intellectum*, "faith seeking understanding". His most famous argument derives a thing's existence from its *definition*. That is the Missing Definition, and his own unspoken principle that what truly existed can't be undone by forgetting | the monastic world of *The Name of the Rose* |
| **Lucan** | From Latin *Lucanus*; it sounds like *lux*, "light", though that's not the true etymology | A warrior of light by sound. And the Roman poet Lucan was a writer silenced by power: Nero forced him to suicide in 65 AD. The Redactors would approve | Coelho's warrior of light; **Lucas** Corso of *The Club Dumas* |
| **Adso** | Germanic, uncertain | Eco's narrator: the novice who survives the fire, gathers the fragments from the ashes, and writes it all down as an old man. Very on the nose if Panagiotis knows the book | *The Name of the Rose* |
| **Theodmund** | Old English *þēod* "people" + *mund* "protection": **protector of the people** | Panagiotis's own word; Nadari already calls him this. If he meant it as the name, it's a good one | Panagiotis |

**My recommendation: Anselm.** It is the only one that serves all three sides of him:
the shield (the helm), the scholar (faith seeking understanding), and the mystery (a
thing's existence argued from its definition). It also sits naturally in a Realms
monastery.

**A family name, if he needs one:** his family could be bookbinders, which would explain
why he reads bindings. In *The Club Dumas* the forger-binders are the brothers
**Ceniza**, Spanish for "ash". A Realms-sounding equivalent ("Ashcote", "Ashbinder") would
be a quiet joke for anyone who knows the book: a family named for ash, and a son who
pulls books out of fires.

## 7. With the bladesinger

See `Bladesinger.md` §8 and `Campaign web.md` §1. The mentor's last instruction
("Find the reader … ask him what he remembers") points at someone the Light remembers.
The elf carries that Light and is missing pieces of his memory, and the Oghmanite's kit
power is built to restore exactly that kind of loss.

**[proposed]** Let them meet as **strangers with a shared riddle**, not as people who
already know each other. The moment the Oghmanite hears "Seek the one who remembers"
from the elf, or the elf hears "ask him what he remembers", is a scene worth saving for
the table.

BACKGROUND

![Oghma's holy symbol: a blank scroll](<OGHMANITE BACKGROUND_files/image1.jpg>)

**“A truth does not cease to exist because the world has forgotten it.”**

**“Every story ever told really happened. Stories are where memories go when they’re forgotten.”**

You are a devoted follower of **Oghma, the Binder of What Is Known**. Oghma is the god of knowledge, inspiration, invention and learning, and his followers include sages, scholars, scribes, cartographers, bards, inventors and all those who seek, preserve or uncover knowledge. His domain encompasses not merely written knowledge, but ideas themselves—even those that exist only within a person's mind.

That last distinction has always fascinated you.

*A book can be destroyed.*

*A library can burn.*

*A monument can be demolished.*

*A civilization can disappear.*

*But an* ***idea*** *can survive.*

It can pass from one mind to another. It can be spoken. Remembered. Recreated.

Oghma's holy symbol is a blank scroll. You have always loved that symbol.

To most people, it represents knowledge waiting to be written. To you, it represents something else: **Possibility.**

The blank space contains everything that has not yet been discovered.

**CHILDHOOD:** You were raised in an environment where books, stories and questions were treated with respect. (θα βρω τι θα βάλουμε για οικογένεια κλπ),

Books were never strange objects to you. They were companions. You learned early that every book represented someone's attempt to preserve something.

A history was an attempt to preserve memory. A map preserved knowledge of a place. A song preserved an emotion. A religious text preserved belief. A journal preserved one person's experience. A mathematical treatise preserved an idea.

And a name written in a book meant: **Someone had thought this person was important enough to remember.**

That fascinated you.

**THE FIRST QUESTION:** You were still young when you encountered your first historical contradiction. You found two accounts of the same event. They disagreed. Not slightly. Completely.

One account claimed that a particular person had been a hero. Another described him as a traitor. You asked your teacher which account was correct.

The answer was: “We don't know.”

That should have ended the matter. It didn't.

You asked: “Then why does one of them say it happened differently?”

Your teacher shrugged. “Because history is complicated.”

You accepted that answer outwardly. But something inside you had already begun to change. You realized that **ignorance leaves traces**.

When something is forgotten, there is often evidence that it was once known.

A missing page.

A damaged inscription.

A name scratched from a monument.

A sudden gap in a chronicle.

A story that changes depending on who tells it.

You became fascinated by those gaps.

**THE THING THAT WAS MISSING:** As you grew older, your interests became increasingly specific. You did not merely enjoy learning. You enjoyed **finding what had been lost**.

You searched for forgotten settlements, disputed historical events, vanished families, destroyed temples, lost manuscripts, contradictory genealogies, unidentified symbols, unexplained inscriptions, abandoned roads, missing names, legends that had survived without their origins.

Other scholars often searched for answers. You became fascinated by the questions that remained, after the answers disappeared. Your favorite discoveries were not necessarily the grand ones.

Sometimes the most interesting thing you found was a single missing sentence or a name appearing in one record but nowhere else or a symbol that had survived after everyone had forgotten what it meant.

You began to understand something that would eventually become central to your life: **What is absent can be evidence too.**

**THE NAME PYLACHEUS:** You first encountered the name **Pylacheus** while investigating an unrelated historical question. He was described as an Ilmatari scholar. A brilliant one. And, according to the sources you found, a **heretic**.

The official story was fairly simple. Pylacheus had once been a devoted follower of Ilmater, via one of his Saints, namely Saint Selimbrar. Then he became obsessed with forbidden subjects. He began investigating ancient mysteries that respectable scholars avoided. He challenged established teachings. He apparently crossed boundaries that should not have been crossed. Eventually his reputation collapsed. His writings were scattered or destroyed. His name survived as an example of a scholar who had allowed his hunger for knowledge to overcome wisdom.

You found the story interesting.

But you also found it suspicious.

There were inconsistencies in the accounts of Pylacheus. References appeared to works that no longer existed. People mentioned in one account disappeared from another. Some of his correspondence seemed to have survived only through quotations. Several documents referred to discoveries he supposedly made without ever explaining what those discoveries were. And, most interestingly, different sources disagreed about **why his writings had disappeared**.

Some claimed the Church had destroyed them. Others suggested Pylacheus himself had destroyed them. A few implied that someone else had deliberately removed them.

You became obsessed. You began searching for the missing pieces. Not because you believed Pylacheus was innocent. You simply wanted to know what had actually happened.

**YOUR MENTOR:** Eventually, someone noticed your work (θα σου πω ποιος κλπ). An older Oghmanyte scholar approached you. He was an archivist, historian and investigator. He had spent decades studying obscure subjects that most people considered irrelevant. He was exactly the sort of person you wanted to become.

He became your mentor.

Under him, you learned that scholarship was not simply the accumulation of facts. It was the discipline of determining **which facts could be trusted**. He taught you how to compare manuscripts. How to recognize copied errors. How to identify alterations. How to distinguish deliberate censorship from ordinary loss. How to determine whether two different stories had actually originated from the same source.

He taught you ancient languages. Old scripts. Historical methodology. And perhaps, most importantly: **Never assume that the surviving version of a story is the complete version.**

Your mentor had a favorite saying: **“When a page is missing, don't only ask what was written on it. Ask who tore it out.”**

**PYLACHEUS AGAIN:** Your mentor eventually became interested in your investigation. He had heard the name Pylacheus before. More importantly, he had investigated him himself. You began working together. Years passed.

Eventually you discovered evidence that changed the entire story. Pylacheus had probably **not been a heretic**.

The evidence suggested that he had remained devoted to his faith. He had not rejected Ilmater and vice versa. He had not abandoned his beliefs. Instead, it appeared that Pylacheus had deliberately allowed history to remember him as a dangerous scholar.

Why? You didn't know.

But your mentor believed that Pylacheus had done something extraordinary.

He had deliberately made his own writings unreliable. He had scattered his research. He had destroyed some of it. He had hidden other pieces. He had apparently communicated his discoveries to other people rather than recording everything in one place. Your mentor eventually gave the phenomenon a name… **The Living Testament.**

The principle was simple. Pylacheus had apparently realized that written knowledge could be destroyed.

*A book could be burned.*

*A scroll could be confiscated.*

*A library could be destroyed.*

*A scholar could be killed.*

*But an idea carried by living people could survive.*

So instead of leaving one complete record, Pylacheus divided his discoveries. One person knew one thing. Another knew something else. Another carried a memory. Another possessed a name. Another knew how the pieces fit together. Perhaps none of them even understood the importance of what they carried.

Your mentor believed this was deliberate.

You found the concept extraordinary. It was almost a perfect expression of Oghma's philosophy.

Knowledge did not belong to books. Books were merely vessels. **Knowledge existed wherever the idea existed.**

And an idea carried in a living mind could survive even when every library containing it had burned.

**YOUR MENTOR'S STRANGE INTEREST IN YOU:** As the years passed, your mentor began asking you unusual questions. He would sometimes ask: “*Have you ever recognized a symbol before seeing it?*” Or: “*Have you ever read a damaged inscription and known what was missing?*” Or: “*Have you ever been certain that two unrelated historical events were connected, even before you could explain why?*”

You assumed he was testing your abilities as a scholar. You were probably right. At least partly. But there was something else.

Your mentor had begun to suspect that **you yourself were somehow connected to the Living Testament**.

He never gave you a satisfactory explanation. You didn't know whether he believed you possessed a piece of Pylacheus's research, had inherited some hidden document, or were simply following the same intellectual path Pylacheus had once followed.

You eventually stopped asking.

**THE MISSING DEFINITION:** There is something peculiar about you. You have an instinct for **definitions**.

Sometimes a word bothers you because it isn't precise enough. Sometimes you encounter a historical account and realize that the author is using a particular term incorrectly. Sometimes you read a passage and immediately understand that the writer has confused two concepts that should remain separate.

You have often corrected scholars more experienced than yourself. You don't always know how you know. You simply recognize that: **Something is being called something it is not.**

You have wondered whether this is simply the result of years of study. Perhaps it is. But there is another possibility. One your mentor never explained.

**THE NAMELESS WORD:** There is a word you have encountered in dreams. You cannot remember it.

You know it has several syllables. You know that it is extremely old. And you know that it is important. But whenever you awaken, the word disappears.

What remains is only a sensation: **Something has been defined incorrectly.**

You have sometimes dreamed that you are standing before an enormous darkness. There is no face. No body. No recognizable creature. Only an absence. And somehow you understand that the darkness is trying to convince you that something never existed.

Then you hear a voice. You never see its speaker. It says: **“Name it.”**

You wake before you can answer.

**YOUR FAITH IN OGHMA:** Your devotion to Oghma is not simply an intellectual obsession. You believe that knowledge has value because ideas change the world.

*A map can save a traveler.*

*A medical text can save a life.*

*A spell can defend a city.*

*A historical account can prevent an old mistake from being repeated.*

*A song can preserve the memory of someone who would otherwise be forgotten.*

*A name can restore someone's place in history.*

*And sometimes, discovering the truth about something can change the course of generations.*

You therefore believe that the pursuit of knowledge is not merely academic. **It is a responsibility.**

You do not believe every secret should immediately be revealed. Some knowledge is dangerous. Some knowledge requires preparation. Some truths can cause more harm when misunderstood than when unknown. Your responsibility is therefore not simply to **find knowledge**.

It is to understand it. Preserve it. And determine what should happen to it.

**YOUR VIEW OF PYLACHEUS:** Your feelings toward Pylacheus have changed over the years. At first, you considered him an interesting historical figure. Then you suspected he had been misunderstood. Eventually, you became convinced that he had deliberately constructed the false history surrounding himself.

You don't know why. But you now believe one thing strongly: **Pylacheus knew something important.**

Something important enough that he decided the world was safer without knowing it all at once. That is a disturbing conclusion for an Oghmanyte. Because your faith teaches you to seek knowledge.

Yet Pylacheus apparently believed that **how knowledge survives can be more important than how much knowledge survives.** That contradiction fascinates you.

**THE NINE:** During your research into Pylacheus, you encountered references to nine extraordinary beings. You have never found a complete account. Their names have survived in fragments. Different documents describe them differently. Some imply they were heroes. Others call them guardians. Others suggest that they sacrificed themselves for some purpose that has since been forgotten.

Their names are: **Salendra, Mistee Addizar, Crafyr, Altumil, Braniver, Zephon, Krivdaar, Galizur, Akalim**

You don't know what united them. You don't know why their history is so fragmented. And you don't know why your mentor became increasingly interested in them. But you are convinced that they are connected to Pylacheus.

**VIRINIS:** There is another name. **Virinis.** An ancient phoenix.

You found the name in connection with the earliest references to the Nine. You have never understood exactly how. But the name produces an unusual reaction in you. The first time you encountered it, you experienced a sudden sensation of certainty. Recognition, rather than knowledge. For an instant, you saw something in your mind: A vast field of light. A great wing. Figures standing together. Then everything vanished.

You dismissed it as imagination. You have never completely believed yourself.

**HEIR OF DUSK:** Your mentor eventually found another name connected to the mystery. **Lareth.**

You know very little about him. His historical record is extraordinarily inconsistent. References to him appear in places where they should not. His story seems to have been interrupted and rewritten several times. Your mentor believed that Lareth was somehow connected to Pylacheus.

Your mentor only referenced the name **Heir of Dusk** and **Lareth as “Beautiful”.** You don't know why.

You asked what that meant. He refused to explain. You asked whether it was a title.

He said: “Perhaps.”

You asked whether it was his real name. He answered: “No.”

You asked what it meant. He simply said: **“You will understand when you meet him.”**

**THE LAST LESSON:** Shortly before his death, your mentor left you one final message.

He wrote: **“When the Missing Definition begins to define itself, seek the one whom the light remembers at dusk.”**

You have read those words hundreds of times. They make no more sense now than they did when you first received them. But you know your mentor. He never wrote riddles for amusement. He believed every word mattered.

**YOUR STRANGE ABILITY:** There are moments when you encounter something and simply **know that its definition is wrong**.

You might find an inscription describing a particular event and immediately sense that the terminology is incorrect. You might encounter a religious symbol and realize that someone has misunderstood what it originally represented. You might read an ancient text and recognize that a crucial concept has been deliberately substituted with another. You don't know how you know.

You have learned not to trust the sensation blindly. Sometimes you're wrong. But sometimes you discover later that you were right.

Your mentor called this: **“The Missing Definition.”**

You thought he meant your scholarly talent. You never realized that he may have been describing something far more literal.

**YOUR GREATEST QUESTION:** You have spent years studying forgotten knowledge. You have found lost documents. You have reconstructed damaged inscriptions. You have corrected historical errors. You have uncovered stories that people believed were gone forever.

But one question keeps returning. It is the question behind everything. **What happens when something is forgotten completely?**

Not when a book is destroyed. Not when a name disappears from a library. Not when a civilization forgets a king. You mean something more fundamental.

If nobody remembers that something ever existed...

Does it matter?

Does the truth remain true?

Can something be completely erased simply by removing every record of it?

You have never found an answer.

And that question has gradually become the center of your life.

**YOUR PERSONALITY:** You are curious. Not casually curious. Dangerously curious.

If someone tells you: “Nobody knows what this means.” you immediately want to know why.

If someone tells you: “That book is forbidden.” your first question is: **“Why?”**

If someone tells you: “That is simply how history happened.” you ask: **“According to whom?”**

You are comfortable admitting that you don't know something. You are much less comfortable with someone insisting that something cannot be known. You have a strong respect for legitimate scholarship.

But you distrust authority that demands obedience without explanation.

**YOUR GREATEST FEAR:** Your greatest fear is not ignorance. Ignorance can be corrected.

Your greatest fear is **the deliberate destruction of knowledge**.

Someone who doesn't know something can learn. Someone who has been prevented from knowing has been robbed. You fear libraries burning. You fear languages disappearing. You fear historians rewriting events for political convenience. You fear ancient cultures becoming nothing more than myths because nobody bothered to preserve their stories.

But above all, you fear something even more disturbing: **That something could be deliberately erased so completely that nobody even remembers that there was ever anything to search for.**

**YOUR GREATEST WEAKNESS:** You cannot leave a mystery alone. An unanswered question occupies your mind. A damaged inscription becomes an irritation. A missing page becomes an obsession. A historical contradiction becomes a problem that must be solved.

You have occasionally endangered yourself because you wanted to know what was behind a locked door. You have spent money you couldn't afford on obscure manuscripts. You have traveled considerable distances because someone mentioned a symbol that resembled one in an ancient text.

You know this is not always wise. You also know that you will probably do it again.

**YOUR PERSONAL CODE:** You have developed several principles over the years. **Seek knowledge.** Ignorance should never be celebrated simply because knowledge is difficult.

**Preserve what can be preserved.** Every lost book is a wound to history.

**Question the obvious.** The surviving account is not necessarily the correct account.

**Do not confuse knowledge with wisdom.** Knowing something does not automatically mean you should use it.

**Do not destroy knowledge simply because it is dangerous.** If something must be hidden, preserve the fact that it exists.

**Do not reveal knowledge simply because you possess it.** Some truths require context.

**Remember that every idea began in someone's mind.** Even a dangerous idea should be understood before it is judged.

And one principle you have never consciously formulated: **If something truly existed, forgetting it should not be enough to make it cease to have existed.**

<u>Your mentor's final note</u> remains also in your possession. You have read it so many times that you know every word. It says: “**Do not search for the book. The book was never the vessel. Knowledge survives where memory survives. Find the reader. Find the one whom the light remembers at dusk. And when you meet him... ask him what he remembers.”**

**THE DREAMS:** Throughout the years, you have experienced various dreams. These are the most prominent, which you still remember to this day.

**Dream I:** You have dreamed of the library for as long as you can remember. You never know where it is. You never remember entering it. You simply find yourself standing inside.

It is enormous. So enormous that you cannot see the far walls.

Shelves rise higher than towers, disappearing into darkness above you. Thousands upon thousands of books fill them. Some are bound in leather. Others in wood, metal, or strange materials you cannot identify.

There are scrolls. Tablets. Stone fragments. Clay cylinders. Loose pages. Books written in languages you recognize and thousands you do not.

And yet everything is perfectly organized. You know this instinctively. There is a place for everything.

Every story. Every name. Every idea. Every memory.

You walk between the shelves. You do not know what you are looking for. But you know you will recognize it when you see it.

You pass countless books. Some have names written upon their spines. Others have symbols. Others are completely blank. Eventually you reach a narrow passage between two enormous shelves. At the end is a single table. Upon it lies an open book.

You approach. The pages are completely white. **White, not empty.** As though the pages themselves are made of light.

You turn the first page. Nothing.

The second. Nothing.

The third. Still nothing.

Then you notice something. There are impressions in the paper. Words have been written there once. But someone has removed the ink. You run your fingers over the page. You can feel the grooves. Someone wrote something here. Something important. You turn another page. More writing has been erased.

Another. More. Another.

Every page has been deliberately emptied.

You suddenly understand that the entire book was once full. And someone has gone to extraordinary lengths to make it appear that it never contained anything. You become angry. You don't know why. You turn the pages faster.

Nothing. Nothing. Nothing.

Then, near the middle of the book, you find one page that has not been erased completely. There is only one word. You cannot read it.

You know that you should be able to.

You stare at it. The letters seem to change. First they resemble a language you know.

Then another. Then another. Then they become symbols. Then shapes. Then something that cannot be written.

You suddenly understand the meaning. Not the word. The **meaning**.

And just as you begin to remember it—**the entire library goes dark.**

Instantly. Every candle. Every lamp. Every glowing manuscript. Everything disappears.

You are standing in complete darkness. You hear pages turning somewhere far away.

Then a voice whispers: **“If it is not written, was it ever known?”**

You try to answer. You cannot speak.

The voice asks again: **“If no one remembers it, was it ever true?”**

You wake before you can answer.

**Dream II:** This dream is different. There is no library. There is only darkness.

You are floating. You cannot feel your body. You cannot move. There is no sound. For what seems like an eternity, there is absolutely nothing.

Then— **something happens.**

A point of light appears. Tiny. Almost insignificant. It is so far away that you could cover it with your hand. But there is something impossible about it. The darkness does not surround the light.

The darkness seems to exist **because the light exists**.

You watch.

The point becomes larger. It becomes brighter. Then brighter still. And suddenly you realize that it isn't getting closer. **Everything else is appearing around it.**

Space. Distance. Color. Shape. Sound.

You see something that looks like a star. Then another. Then thousands. Then millions. You see worlds. You see oceans. Mountains. Forests. Cities. Creatures.

You see things you recognize. And things that you cannot possibly recognize. Everything is coming from that single point.

But then something goes wrong. A darkness appears at the edge of your vision. Not ordinary darkness. Something deeper. Something that seems to have no shape because it is trying to erase the idea of shape itself.

Where it touches the stars, they simply...**cease.**

You watch one disappear. You know that it existed.

Then another disappears. And another.

The darkness moves toward the light. You feel terror, because you understand that if the darkness reaches the light, something much worse than destruction will occur.

You hear a voice. It may be yours. It may belong to someone else.

It says: **“Remember.”**

The light grows brighter. The darkness recoils.

Then you see something impossible. Figures are standing between the light and the darkness. You cannot see their faces. There are several of them. You try to count.

One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six—

The dream becomes blurred. You cannot see the others.

One figure turns toward you. You cannot see their face. But you know they are looking directly at you. They raise one hand.

And you hear: **“Do not let it be forgotten.”**

The light suddenly becomes overwhelming. You wake. For several seconds after waking, you can still see a pale glow in the darkness of your room.

Then it disappears.

**Dream III:** This is the dream that begins after your mentor’s last death.

You are standing on a hill. The sun is setting. Everything is bathed in red and gold. You are holding a book. You don't remember bringing it.

You open it. Every page is blank. You turn another. Blank. Another. Blank.

Then you hear footsteps behind you. You turn. A man is standing several paces away. You cannot see his face. He is dressed in dark armor. There is something terribly wrong about him.

Something about him feels like a contradiction. Like a dead thing that remembers being alive.

You ask: **“Who are you?”**

He does not answer. You ask again. He looks at the book in your hands.

Then he says: **“You carry the question.”**

You look down. The book has changed. There is now writing on the page.

Four words: **WHAT DID THEY PRESERVE?**

You look up. The man is closer.

You ask: **“What are you talking about?”**

He answers: **“You already know.”**

You shake your head. “No.”

He looks almost sad. **“You know the question.”**

He places one hand upon the book. The pages begin turning by themselves.

Faster. Faster. Faster.

You see fragments: A woman praying…. Figures…A great light….A darkness beneath everything….a face you almost recognize….A symbol you have seen before….A name.

You try to read it. The letters change. The man suddenly grabs your wrist. His hand is impossibly cold.

He says: **“Do not remember the name.”**

You stare at him. “Why?”

His face is still hidden. But you somehow know that he is afraid.

He whispers: **“Because it remembers you.”**

The dream shatters. You wake suddenly. Your heart is racing. Your hand hurts. For several moments you can still feel the pressure of the man's fingers around your wrist.

Then you realize there is nothing there.

# **<u>THE SHIELD OF THE SCROLL</u>**

![Shield of the Scroll emblem: a longsword over a blue scroll, chains breaking around a black-and-white shield](<OGHMANITE BACKGROUND_files/image2.jpg>)

"A sword may kill a man. Fire may destroy a library. But ignorance can kill a civilization."

You were raised to **remember**, rather become a warrior. From your earliest years, you were taught that knowledge was not merely something to possess. It was something to protect.

A forgotten name was a wound.

A destroyed manuscript was a death.

A deliberately falsified history was an act of violence against generations yet unborn.

You came to understand this through the teachings of Oghma and, more specifically, through the **Children of the Passive Voice**.

The Children observed, gathered, copied and preserved.

They guarded the libraries and abbeys, and when knowledge was lost, they searched for it. Their philosophy was simple. **Do not alter history merely because you dislike it.**

Record what happened. Preserve what survives. Seek what has been lost. And never allow your own beliefs to become an excuse for rewriting the truth.

That philosophy gave the order its name. The **Passive Voice**.

For generations, it served them well. Until it nearly destroyed them.

THE SCHISM OF SECRETS: Centuries ago, the Children discovered that remaining neutral did not necessarily mean remaining innocent. A network of historical revisionists calling themselves the **Redactors** had infiltrated libraries throughout Faerûn.

They did not merely steal books. They altered them. They removed names. Changed dates. Destroyed genealogies. Replaced accounts. Burned inconvenient histories.

Among the most heavily targeted records were accounts of ancient elven magical catastrophes and blights.

The Redactors understood something the Children had failed to appreciate: **If you control the surviving record of history, you can change what the living believe happened.**

The Children initially refused to interfere. That was their doctrine. Observe. Preserve. Do not take sides.

But eventually the monks discovered that some of the libraries they were sworn to protect had already been infiltrated.

Their neutrality was being used against them. They were watching history being murdered while insisting that they were merely observers. The result became known within the order as: **The Schism of Secrets.**

The old doctrine survived. But alongside it arose an unspoken principle. **Knowledge must sometimes be defended before it can be preserved.**

The senior clerics began quietly identifying individuals capable of doing something the traditional monks could not. Individuals who could fight. Investigate. Track. Recover.

And, when necessary, kill those who intended to destroy knowledge permanently. They were not knights. They were not crusaders. They were not supposed to impose Oghma's truth upon the world. They existed for a narrower purpose.

**Protect the truth from those who would erase it.**

These warriors became known informally as: **Shields of the Scroll**

You are one of them.

**YOUR TRAINING**: You learned languages alongside swordsmanship. You studied ancient dialects while learning how to fight. You were taught how to identify manuscripts by their ink, parchment, binding, handwriting, and provenance. You learned how scribes conceal alterations. How thieves remove pages without leaving obvious traces. How to determine whether a burned manuscript was destroyed deliberately or accidentally. How to recognize a false copy. How to reconstruct missing passages.

And how to defend yourself when the people who want a book destroyed discover that someone is trying to save it. Your weapon became the **longsword**, an appropriate expression of your calling and, in your tradition, associated with Oghma. But your sword was never your most important weapon.

Your memory was. Your questions were. Your ability to recognize when something **did not belong** was. Your instructors noticed something unusual about you. You were very good at finding contradictions. If two accounts disagreed, you could not simply choose one.

You wanted to know: **Why?** Who had written each version? Who had copied it? Who had benefited from the alteration? Who had disappeared from the record? And, most importantly: **Who wanted you to believe the wrong version?**

That question eventually became the foundation of your life.

**YOUR FIRST REAL FAILURE:** Your first encounters with the man who would eventually become your nemesis, involved a stolen text.

And a man named **Corvus**. Corvus the Redactor. You had heard his name before. At first, only as a rumor. A thief. A cultist. A murderer. A man who appeared wherever forbidden religious texts disappeared.

You encountered him once before. You survived. But you did not understand him.

You would later come to understand that Corvus did not steal knowledge because he desired knowledge. He stole it because he understood something terrifying: **Knowledge could be turned into a weapon.**

**THE LEDGER OF GRACE:** Your second encounter with Corvus came while you were pursuing missing religious lore in a plague-quarantined village on the outskirts of Tethyr. The village was dying. Its gates partially sealed. The roads were watched. The sick were confined.

And somewhere inside the village temple, the local Ilmatari clerics were protecting something that had nothing to do with the plague. A forbidden manuscript.

A sealed codex known as: The Ledger of Grace.

You had been sent to investigate it because the Children had learned that the book contained an extraordinary record. It contained Names. Hundreds of them. The true genealogical names and bloodlines of historic martyrs who had died fighting dark cults in the name of **Saint Selimbrar**. Bloodlines that had survived for centuries.

The book was essentially a living genealogical map of heroic Ilmatari families. To you, it was sacred history. To Corvus, it was something else. A **hit list**.

He did not merely want the book destroyed. He intended to use it. You learned too late what he planned… If he could identify the descendants of those ancient martyrs, he could hunt them down one by one.

Kill them. Destroy their families. Erase their bloodlines.

And with them, erase the living continuation of those who had once opposed the darkness.

But you feared, Corvus had another purpose. The Ledger's pages had become the focus of a horrific **Bhaalist ritual**.

You arrived at the temple too late. The sanctuary had already been desecrated. Bodies lay among broken benches. Holy symbols had been defaced. Blood had been smeared across the altar. And Corvus stood there.

The final index of names was burning.

You remember the smell. Burning parchment. Ink. Blood.

You charged.

For one moment, you thought you had him. Then the ritual erupted. Darkness swallowed the temple. Corvus disappeared into it.

You reached the altar. And there was the choice. You could pursue him. Or save the book.

You knew what the Children had taught you. A criminal could be hunted again. A stolen book could perhaps be recovered.

But the names on those pages— Those names were irreplaceable. So you chose the knowledge.

You reached into the flames. The parchment burned your hands. You pulled the Ledger away from the ritual before the remaining names could be consumed.

Corvus escaped. You had failed to capture him. But you had preserved the bloodlines.

You told yourself that was victory. You were not entirely convinced.

**CORVUS**: After Tethyr, Corvus ceased being merely an enemy. He became a question.

Why was he so interested in religious genealogies? Why Bhaal? Why Ilmater? Why destroy the descendants of ancient martyrs? And why use sacred texts as components of rituals?

You began collecting every fragment of information you could find. Most were contradictory. Some were obviously false. Others seemed deliberately incomplete. That was when you began noticing something disturbing.

**Corvus wasn't simply looking for books.** He was looking for **specific knowledge**. And someone was feeding him clues.

**ASBRAVN**: Six years ago, your duties took you to **Asbravn**, a prosperous farming town in the fertile fields of Sunset Vale. Your steps led you there, to the local Ilmatari temple, the **House of the Suffering God**, under the rumor that they possessed a rare collection of theological manuscripts.

They had been written generations earlier by a high scribe named: **Egen Kantorius.**

Kantorius had meticulously recorded the suppressed teachings of an ancient Ilmatari scholar: **Pylacheus the Heretic.**

You knew the name. Pylacheus was supposed to have been a dangerous theologian. A heretic. A man whose ideas had gone too far. His writings had been suppressed. His name was treated with suspicion.

Yet the Kantorius manuscripts suggested something stranger. Pylacheus had argued that suffering was not merely something to be endured.

It was a **cosmic mirror**.

Suffering revealed the consequences of violence. It exposed the futility of power. It showed what happened when beings—mortal or divine—attempted to impose their will upon existence through destruction. And, most controversially, Pylacheus had written about powers such as Bhaal.

He believed that suffering could reveal the **futility of gods who defined themselves through violence**.

You had been fascinated. You had intended to spend several weeks studying the collection. You never got the chance.

You arrived at the temple gates at dusk. The doors were open. That was the first thing you noticed. The second was the silence. Then you smelled blood. You entered. And found a slaughterhouse.

The young acolytes had been murdered where they stood. Some had been killed while praying. Others had attempted to flee. Blood covered the stone floor. The sanctuary had been desecrated.

And at the altar stood **Corvus**. He was tearing the Kantorius manuscripts from their iron cases.

You recognized him immediately. He looked at you. And smiled. "You again."

You drew your longsword. You charged. The battle was savage. You cut down two of his cultists. For a moment you thought you might finally have him.

But Corvus was not a conventional fighter. He moved like a thief. Like a predator. Dark magic flickered around him.

He slipped past your guard and drove a jagged sacrificial dagger into your thigh. The pain was blinding. Your leg collapsed. You fell. And from the floor you watched him take the manuscripts.

You tried to rise. You couldn't. Corvus looked down at you. Then he smiled. He tucked the stolen texts beneath his robes. And disappeared into the shadows. Again.

You had failed. But this time the cost was unbearable. The dead were not abstract names in a ledger.

They were people you had seen. Young acolytes. People who had been praying when they died.

You dragged yourself through the desecrated shrine. And then you saw something. Written in blood.

The words were broken and incomplete:

**"Kantorius knew....bind the Blood-Lord's shadow...**

**...seeks the source text...forbidden tome of Bhaalist heresy was smuggled away from the vaults...**

**...hidden...Ilmatari monk...**

**...before the ink is turned to blood."**

You read it again. And again. There was something hidden inside those words. You could feel it. But you didn't know what.

Who was the **Ilmatari monk**? What was the **source text**? What did Kantorius know? What did "bind the Blood-Lord's shadow" mean? And why was Corvus willing to slaughter an entire temple to obtain those writings?

<u>THE WOUND</u>: Your physical wound eventually healed. The other wound did not. You had failed to save the manuscripts. You had failed to stop Corvus.

And worst of all, you had arrived at the House of the Suffering God believing that you were investigating knowledge. You left realizing that **someone else had been investigating you**.

Corvus now knew that you would pursue him. You knew that he would continue searching. And somewhere between the two of you lay a body of knowledge neither of you yet understood.

From that day forward, Corvus became your nemesis, because he had shown you something you could never forget: **There are people who do not want knowledge for the sake of knowing.**

They want to control what the world is allowed to remember.

<u>THE YEARS AFTER ASBRAVN</u>: You returned to the Children. You reported everything. The Ledger of Grace. Corvus. The Kantorius manuscripts. Pylacheus. The blood-written message. The mysterious source text. The supposed Ilmatari monk.

The response from your superiors was cautious. Perhaps too cautious. You were told to continue investigating. But quietly.

You began searching archives. Ilmatari records. Old Oghmanyte indexes. Fragments of theological correspondence. References to Pylacheus. Mentions of Kantorius. Cross-references to Bhaalist texts.

You found almost nothing. And what you did find was inconsistent.

Names disappeared. Dates contradicted one another. Books that supposedly existed had no surviving copies. People mentioned in one account vanished from another. You began to recognize a pattern. The same pattern you had been trained to detect since childhood.

Someone—or several someones— had been **removing pieces of the record**.

## THE LETTER OF THAELITH

**Khelben,**

If you are reading this, then I have either lost the argument I have been having with time, or decided that there are some things I would rather entrust to you than to silence.

There is a child in the Forest of Mir. He is of House Dahast. He was born without a name.

Do not ask me why. I have spent many years asking the same question. I no longer believe the answer belongs to me.

There is, however, another matter. The child bears a mark. I have searched elven records for it. I have found nothing. I have searched the records of Cormanthyr. Nothing. I have searched the oldest accounts of Myth Drannor. Nothing.

Then, by chance—or by the peculiar cruelty of chance—I found it in a record that should never have contained it.

**A dwarven record.**

The symbol appears beside a phrase I cannot properly translate.

The dwarves called it **Ruan Famkul**.

I think it means something closer to **that which must be made forgotten.**

And then I found something else. A reference to a place where elves had no business being. It was written among records concerning the final days of **Delzoun**.

The account speaks of a journey north. Beyond the old roads. Beyond the settlements. Into the mountains where the dwarves had begun carrying their treasures away from the dying kingdom.

The final passage is damaged.

I can read only this: **"...where the anvils froze beneath the mountain..."**

and: **"...the road into the valley was closed..."**

and: **"...the axe must not..."**

The rest is gone.

But there is one sentence that remains. I have read it perhaps fifty times. I still do not understand it.

**"They came from the west, though no elf had crossed the passes."**

Khelben, if you know anything of the last days of Delzoun, you know why this troubles me. There should have been no elves there. Yet the record insists that there were.

And beside the passage is the same mark carried by the child.

I do not know what happened in that valley. I do not know what the elves were doing there. I do not know what the dwarves hid.

But I have become convinced of one thing: **The child did not inherit that mark from his mother.**

His mother carried nothing like it. It was already upon him when he was born. And I do not believe it is a mark of his blood.

I believe it is a mark of something that happened **before he had a name.**

If the mark ever changes, if the child ever sees the symbol somewhere outside himself, or if anyone tells him the words **Ruan Famkul**, then you must do something I have rarely asked of anyone.

**Do not let him go north alone.**

Find the dwarves. Find those who remember the last days of Delzoun.

And if you find the valley mentioned in the record, do not assume that what was hidden there was hidden because it was evil.

Some things are forgotten because they are dangerous.

Others are forgotten because remembering them changes the world.

I do not yet know which this is. Perhaps the boy will.

**Thaelith**
